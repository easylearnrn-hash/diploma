🚨 High-Alert Medications & Safety Protocols

Drugs with high risk of serious harm if used incorrectly — double-check, use smart pumps, follow protocols

🚨 Common High-Alert Medications

  • Insulin — double-check dose and type; use sliding scale; check BG before; hypoglycemia risk
  • Heparin — check aPTT; risk of HIT (heparin-induced thrombocytopenia); antidote = protamine sulfate
  • Chemotherapy — handle with PPE (gown + gloves); extravasation risk; monitor CBC
  • Concentrated electrolytes (KCl, hypertonic saline) — must be diluted; NEVER IV push potassium
  • Opioids (morphine, fentanyl) — respiratory depression; keep Narcan at bedside
  • Warfarin — monitor INR; narrow therapeutic window; antidote = Vitamin K
🧠 NCLEX KEY: High-alert meds require independent double-check by 2 nurses before administration.

📋 Drug Safety Systems

  • Tall man lettering — DOPamine vs. DOBUTamine; HydrALAzine vs. HYDROmorphone
  • Black box warnings — FDA's strongest warning; highest risk of serious adverse effects
  • Smart infusion pumps — dose limits prevent dangerous drip rates
  • Bar code medication administration (BCMA) — scan patient + medication to verify
  • Look-alike/sound-alike (LASA) drugs — stored separately, labeled clearly

⚡ NCLEX Must-Know

  • High-alert meds = insulin, heparin, chemo, concentrated electrolytes, opioids
  • Know antidote pairs — Narcan/opioids, Protamine/heparin, Vitamin K/warfarin, Calcium gluconate/Mg
  • NEVER give potassium IV push → cardiac arrest
  • Monitor trough levels for Vancomycin and Gentamicin
  • Tall man lettering helps differentiate LASA drugs
