Pressure Ulcers (Bedsores)

1. What Is a Pressure Ulcer?

A pressure ulcer (decubitus ulcer, bedsore) is skin damage caused by prolonged pressure that reduces blood flow, leading to tissue breakdown.

Common in: bedridden, immobile, or poorly nourished patients.

Causes & Risk Factors

  • Prolonged pressure — lying/sitting in one position
  • Friction — rubbing against bed or clothing
  • Shearing — skin slides while body moves (e.g., sliding in bed)
  • Moisture — urine, sweat, stool weakens skin (incontinent patients)
  • Poor nutrition — low protein, vitamins, dehydration
  • Medical conditions — diabetes, poor circulation, spinal cord injury

2. Common Locations

Bony prominences (where skin is thin over bone):

  • Back of head (occiput)
  • Shoulders & shoulder blades
  • Elbows
  • Sacrum & coccyx (most common)
  • Hips & buttocks (greater trochanter)
  • Heels & ankles
Heels and sacrum = highest risk areas in bedridden patients.

4. Prevention — Best Strategy

  • ✅ Reposition every 2 hours — most important intervention
  • ✅ Use pillows or foam pads at bony prominences
  • ✅ Keep skin clean and dry
  • ✅ Apply barrier cream (zinc oxide, petroleum jelly) for incontinence
  • ✅ Encourage high-protein diet (protein heals skin)
  • ✅ Use special mattresses (low-air-loss, foam) to redistribute pressure
  • ✅ Assess skin daily, especially over bony areas
Repositioning every 2 hours = #1 prevention priority on NCLEX.

5. Treatment by Stage

Stage 1 & 2 (Mild–Moderate)

  • Keep area clean and dry
  • Moisture-retaining dressings: hydrocolloid, transparent film
  • Reposition frequently; encourage movement

Stage 3 & 4 (Severe / Deep)

  • Debridement — surgical or via dressings (removes dead tissue)
  • Wound dressings:
    • Hydrocolloid — moderate drainage
    • Foam dressings — heavy drainage
    • Alginate — excessive drainage
  • Antibiotic ointment if infected
  • Wound VAC (negative pressure wound therapy) — promotes healing in deep wounds
  • Skin grafting for large or non-healing ulcers

7. NCLEX Must-Know Summary

  • Stage 1 = Red, intact skin → reposition every 2 hours
  • Stage 2 = Blister or shallow wound → moist dressings
  • Stage 3 = Deep crater to fat → special wound care
  • Stage 4 = Muscle/bone exposed → may need surgery
  • Unstageable = debridement needed before staging
  • DTI = purple/maroon, intact skin, very painful = warning sign
  • Reposition every 2 hours = best prevention
  • High-protein diet = speeds wound healing
  • Fever + worsening ulcer = possible sepsis → notify provider
Stage 4 ulcers can lead to osteomyelitis (bone infection) → high mortality risk.
Nutrition tip: Adequate protein (1.2–1.5 g/kg/day), vitamin C, and zinc are critical for wound healing.
