Common Skin Disorders

1. Psoriasis

Chronic autoimmune skin condition → rapid skin cell turnover.

  • Thick, red plaques with silvery-white scales
  • Common on elbows, knees, scalp
  • Not contagious

Treatment

  • Topical corticosteroids
  • Vitamin D analogs (calcipotriene)
  • Phototherapy (UV light)
  • Biologics for severe cases
Avoid triggers: stress, cold, trauma, infection. Teach patient to moisturize and use gentle soaps.

2. Eczema (Atopic Dermatitis)

Chronic inflammatory disorder — often linked to allergies or asthma.

  • Dry, itchy, inflamed skin
  • Common in infants (cheeks) and skin creases (elbows, knees)

Treatment

  • Hydration — thick petroleum-based moisturizers
  • Antihistamines for itching
  • Topical corticosteroids (short-term)
  • Avoid triggers: soaps, allergens, heat
Moisturize within 3 minutes of bathing to lock in moisture.

3. Contact Dermatitis

Localized reaction from skin contact with an allergen or irritant.

  • Red rash, blisters, itching at contact site
  • Common causes: poison ivy, latex, detergents

Treatment

  • Remove the offending agent immediately
  • Topical corticosteroids
  • Cool compresses
Atopic = inherited, chronic, widespread | Contact = direct exposure, localized

4. Scabies

Parasitic infestation by Sarcoptes scabiei mite burrowing under the skin.

  • Severe itching, worse at night
  • Burrow lines (tiny thread-like tracks)
  • Between fingers, wrists, waist

Treatment

  • Permethrin 5% cream — apply neck to toe, rinse after 8–14 hrs
  • Wash clothing/linens in hot water
  • Treat all household members at the same time
Contact precautions until treatment is complete.

5. Impetigo

Highly contagious bacterial skin infection (Staph or Strep).

  • Honey-colored crusts around nose/mouth
  • Common in children after minor skin trauma

Treatment

  • Topical antibiotics (mupirocin)
  • Oral antibiotics if severe (cephalexin)
  • Good hygiene to prevent spread
No school until 24 hours after starting antibiotics.

6. Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS)

Rare, life-threatening skin reaction — usually drug-induced.

Common culprits: sulfa drugs, anticonvulsants, NSAIDs, allopurinol.

  • Painful red/purple rash that blisters and spreads
  • Mucosal involvement: mouth, eyes, genitals
  • Skin sloughs off (looks like a burn)
EMERGENCY: Stop offending drug immediately. Treat like a burn patient (fluids, infection prevention, pain control).

7. Skin Cancer — ABCDE Rule

TypeCauseAppearanceKey Risk
Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC)UV radiationWaxy, pearly bump; slow-growingMost common; rarely metastasizes
Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC)Sun, chronic wounds, HPVScaly red patch or ulcer; can spreadCan metastasize if untreated
MelanomaGenetic + UVIrregular dark mole; changes in size/color/shapeMost deadly; urgent referral

ABCDE Rule

  • A — Asymmetry
  • B — Border irregularity
  • C — Color variation (multiple colors)
  • D — Diameter >6 mm (pencil eraser)
  • E — Evolving (growing, itching, bleeding)
Any mole matching ABCDE criteria → urgent dermatology referral.
