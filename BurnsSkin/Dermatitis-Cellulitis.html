Dermatitis & Cellulitis

1. What Is Dermatitis?

Dermatitis = Inflammation of the skin caused by irritants, allergens, or underlying conditions. Not contagious.

TypeCauseSymptoms
Atopic Dermatitis (Eczema)Genetic, allergies, asthmaItchy, dry, red, scaly patches (face, hands, elbows, knees)
Contact DermatitisIrritant/allergen (soaps, chemicals, poison ivy)Red rash, itching, burning, blisters if severe
Seborrheic DermatitisYeast overgrowth (oily skin, stress)Greasy, yellowish, scaly patches (scalp, face, chest)
Stasis DermatitisPoor blood flow in legsSwollen, discolored skin, open sores on lower legs

2. Dermatitis Treatment

  • Avoid triggers — irritating soaps, chemicals, allergens
  • Moisturize daily with fragrance-free lotions
  • Topical steroids (hydrocortisone, betamethasone) → reduces redness & itching
  • Antihistamines (diphenhydramine/Benadryl) → for itching
  • Seborrheic dermatitis: antifungal shampoos (ketoconazole, selenium sulfide)
  • Stasis dermatitis: elevate legs, compression stockings
Dermatitis = inflammation, not infection → antibiotics NOT indicated unless secondary infection develops.

3. What Is Cellulitis?

Cellulitis = Bacterial skin infection spreading through deeper skin layers. Common organisms: Streptococcus and Staphylococcus.

Entry via: cut, wound, insect bite, athlete's foot, skin breakdown.

Not contagious person-to-person, but can spread inside the body → sepsis or osteomyelitis if untreated.

4. Signs & Symptoms of Cellulitis

  • Red, swollen, warm, tender skin (often leg)
  • Redness spreads quickly
  • Fever and chills (if systemic)
  • Skin may blister or develop pus
  • Lymphangitis — red streaks moving toward the heart = EMERGENCY 🚨
Mark the borders of redness with a skin marker to monitor spread over time.

5. Cellulitis Treatment

Mild Cases (Outpatient)

  • Cephalexin (Keflex) or Dicloxacillin — first-line
  • Clindamycin or Doxycycline — if MRSA suspected

Severe / Spreading Cases (Hospital)

  • Vancomycin — for MRSA
  • Ceftriaxone — for severe Strep
  • Elevate affected extremity (reduces swelling)
  • Pain control: acetaminophen or ibuprofen
Go to ER immediately if:
  • Red streaks appear (lymphangitis)
  • High fever, confusion, dizziness (sepsis)
  • Redness rapidly expands despite antibiotics

6. NCLEX Priorities

Dermatitis

  • ✅ Avoid scratching → prevents secondary infection
  • ✅ Moisturize to prevent dry/cracked skin
  • ✅ Antihistamines for severe itching
  • ✅ Teach trigger avoidance (allergens, harsh soaps)

Cellulitis

  • ✅ Monitor for red streaks or worsening symptoms
  • ✅ Elevate affected area
  • ✅ Complete the full course of antibiotics
  • ✅ Monitor for signs of sepsis (↓BP, fever, confusion)
NCLEX Must-Know:
Dermatitis = inflammation → NO antibiotics
Cellulitis = bacterial → NEEDS antibiotics
Red streaks on skin = lymphangitis = call provider STAT
