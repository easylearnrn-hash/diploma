Virchow's Triad (DVT)

  • Venous Stasis — slow blood flow (immobility, prolonged bed rest)
  • Hypercoagulability — increased clotting tendency (cancer, OCP, dehydration)
  • Endothelial Injury — vessel wall damage (surgery, IV lines, trauma)
All 3 factors = highest DVT risk (e.g., post-surgical patient on bed rest).

DVT — Nursing Priorities

  • Monitor for PE: sudden chest pain, SOB, hemoptysis, ↓O₂ sat → emergency
  • Do NOT massage affected leg (dislodges clot → PE)
  • Elevate leg (if ordered); encourage ambulation when stable
  • Compression stockings (NOT during acute DVT)
  • Anticoagulants: monitor INR (warfarin target 2.0–3.0), aPTT (heparin)
  • Bleeding precautions while on anticoagulants
PE warning signs: Sudden SOB, chest pain, hemoptysis, ↓O₂ sat → STAT O₂, call provider, imaging now.

Cellulitis — Nursing Priorities

  • Mark borders with a pen to monitor progression
  • Elevate limb to reduce swelling
  • Administer antibiotics on time; complete the full course
  • Monitor for sepsis: ↓BP, ↑HR, fever, confusion
  • Educate on wound care and when to return (red streaks = lymphangitis)
  • Warm compresses for comfort
Red streaks moving toward the heart = lymphangitis → call provider STAT.

NCLEX Key Words

DVT Key Words
  • Unilateral leg swelling
  • Pain with walking/standing
  • Post-op / immobile
  • Anticoagulants
  • PE symptoms
  • D-dimer elevated
Cellulitis Key Words
  • Red, hot, swollen skin
  • Localized bacterial infection
  • Antibiotics
  • Fever / wound/insect bite
  • Red streaks → sepsis risk
  • Mark borders to monitor
