喙 1. WHAT IS CARDIOMYOPATHY?  
Cardiomyopathy = a disease of the heart muscle that makes it weaker,  
stretched, or stiff, affecting the heart’s ability to pump blood effectively.  
Over time, this can lead to:  
• Heart failure (when the heart can’t pump enough blood)  
• Dysrhythmias (abnormal heart rhythms)  
• Sudden cardiac death  
 2. TYPES OF CARDIOMYOPATHY  
(ALL TESTED ON NCLEX!)  
A. DILATED CARDIOMYOPATHY (MOST COMMON)  
• Heart chambers stretch out (dilate), especially the left ventricle  
• Muscle becomes thin and weak  
• ↓ Ejection fraction (amount of blood pumped out with each beat)  
Causes:  
• Genetics  
• Alcohol or drug use  
• Viral infections  
• Chemotherapy  
• Pregnancy  
B. HYPERTROPHIC CARDIOMYOPATHY  
• Heart muscle thickens (especially the septum = wall between ventricles)  
• Makes it hard for blood to leave the heart  
• Can block blood flow (called obstructive type)  
Causes:  
• Genetic (inherited from parents)  
• Common in young athletes → can cause sudden death  
C. RESTRICTIVE CARDIOMYOPATHY  
• Heart muscle becomes stiff  
• Can’t stretch to fill with blood  
• Blood backs up into the lungs or body  
Causes:  
• Amyloidosis (protein buildup in organs)  
• Sarcoidosis (inflammatory disease)  
• Radiation therapy  
D. ARRHYTHMOGENIC RIGHT VENTRICULAR  
CARDIOMYOPATHY (ARVC)  
• Right ventricle muscle is replaced with fat/scar tissue  
• Leads to dangerous arrhythmias (irregular heartbeats)  
 3. PATHOPHYSIOLOGY  
No matter the type:  
• ↓ Pumping ability  
• ↓ Cardiac output (amount of blood pumped per minute)  
• Leads to:  
◦ Fatigue  
◦ Shortness of breath  
◦ Fluid buildup  
◦ Risk of heart failure or sudden death  
⚠ 4. SYMPTOMS (WHAT YOU'LL SEE)  
All types can lead to similar heart failure symptoms:  
• Dyspnea (shortness of breath)  
• Fatigue (extreme tiredness)  
• Orthopnea (trouble breathing when lying flat)  
• Paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea (sudden breathlessness at night)  
• Edema (swelling, often in legs)  
• Ascites (fluid buildup in abdomen)  
• Jugular vein distention (visible neck veins = fluid backup)  
• Syncope (fainting, especially in hypertrophic type)  
• Palpitations (feeling of heart racing)  
 5. DIAGNOSIS  
• Echocardiogram (ultrasound of heart) → shows heart size & function  
• Chest X-ray → shows enlarged heart  
• ECG (Electrocardiogram) → detects arrhythmias  
• Cardiac MRI → detailed imaging  
• BNP (Brain Natriuretic Peptide) → ↑ in heart failure  
• Genetic testing → for hypertrophic/ARVC  
離 6. LABS TO MONITOR  
• BNP ↑ (shows stretching of heart)  
• Electrolytes (especially potassium, sodium)  
• Creatinine & BUN (kidney function)  
• Troponin (if chest pain present – shows heart damage)  
 7. TREATMENT  
MEDICATIONS:  
• ACE inhibitors (e.g., lisinopril) → relax blood vessels  
• Beta blockers (e.g., metoprolol) → slow HR, reduce strain  
• Diuretics (e.g., furosemide) → remove fluid  
• Anticoagulants → prevent blood clots  
• Antiarrhythmics → stabilize heart rhythm  
DEVICES:  
• ICD (Implantable Cardioverter-Defibrillator)  
→ shocks the heart if dangerous rhythm occurs  
• Pacemaker  
→ helps control slow or irregular rhythms  
• LVAD (Left Ventricular Assist Device)  
→ helps pump blood in severe cases  
• Heart transplant → if no other treatment works  
啕 8. NURSING INTERVENTIONS  
Monitor for heart failure symptoms  
• Lung sounds: crackles (fluid in lungs)  
• Weight gain = fluid buildup  
• Edema, JVD (jugular vein distention)  
• ↓ Urine output  
• Atelectasis (collapse of lung area from fluid or pressure)  
Daily weights  
→ report 2–3 lbs gain/day or 5 lbs/week  
Elevate HOB (head of bed) → eases breathing  
Oxygen therapy if needed  
Low-sodium diet, fluid restriction  
Balance activity & rest  
Teach patient to report:  
• Chest pain  
• Dizziness or fainting  
• Weight gain  
• Trouble breathing at night  
Medication adherence → prevent complications

易 10. NCLEX PRIORITIES  
• First action for new dyspnea? → Raise HOB  
• What lab confirms HF? → ↑ BNP  
• Priority teaching for hypertrophic cardiomyopathy? → Avoid exercise  
• Best indicator of fluid overload? → Daily weight  
• Sudden collapse in athlete? → Think hypertrophic cardiomyopathy  
✅ 11. FINAL NCLEX TAKEAWAYS  
• Know the types and unique risks of each  
• Focus on safety, medication teaching, fluid monitoring  
• NCLEX loves questions about heart failure symptoms, activity limits,  
and sudden death prevention
