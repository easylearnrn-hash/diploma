Heart Attack vs. Heart Failure vs. Cardiac Arrest  
1. Definitions  
Heart Attack (Myocardial Infarction - MI)  
Cause: Blocked blood flow to the heart muscle  
Result: Heart muscle dies due to lack of oxygen  
Key term:  
•
•
Myocardial = heart muscle  
Infarction = blocked blood supply → tissue death  
Heart Failure  
Cause: Heart is too weak or stiff to pump blood well  
Result: Blood doesn’t meet the body’s needs  
Can affect: Left side, right side, or both sides of the heart  
Not a sudden event—it’s chronic and progressive  
Cardiac Arrest  
Cause: Electrical system failure in the heart  
Result: Heart stops beating completely → no blood flow  
Sudden collapse, no pulse, no breathing  
Common rhythms:  
•
•
•
Ventricular Fibrillation (VF) = quivering, no pumping  
Ventricular Tachycardia (VT) = too fast to fill or pump  
Asystole = flatline, no electrical activity  
2. Pathophysiology  
Condition  
What Happens  
Heart Attack  
Heart Failure  
Cardiac Arrest  
Blood supply is blocked, usually by a clot in the coronary arteries  
Heart can’t pump well → blood backs up or doesn’t reach organs  
Heart’s electrical signals fail → complete stop of heart beat  
3. Causes  
Heart Attack:  
Main cause: Atherosclerosis (fatty plaque buildup in arteries)  
Blockage → clot forms → blood can’t reach part of the heart  
Risk factors:  
•
•
•
•
•
Hypertension (high BP)  
Smoking  
High cholesterol  
Diabetes  
Lack of exercise  
Heart Failure:  
Often caused after a heart attack (dead heart tissue becomes scar)  
Other causes:  
•
•
•
Long-term high BP  
Valve problems (like aortic stenosis)  
Lung diseases (e.g., COPD → right-sided failure)  
Cardiac Arrest:  
Often caused by:  
•
•
•
•
•
Heart attack  
Long-standing heart failure  
Drug overdose  
Drowning  
Electrical disturbance

5. Diagnosis  
Heart Attack:  
•
•
•
ECG (shows ST changes)  
Blood tests (troponin)  
Coronary angiogram  
Heart Failure:  
•
•
•
Chest X-ray (shows fluid)  
BNP test (↑ with heart strain)  
Echocardiogram (checks pumping ability)  
Cardiac Arrest:  
•
•
Clinical exam (unconscious, no pulse)  
ECG (check for VF, VT, or asystole)  
6. Treatment  
Heart Attack (MI):  
Immediate steps:  
•
•
•
•
•
Aspirin (prevents clot)  
Nitroglycerin (opens vessels)  
Morphine (relieves pain, lowers demand)  
Beta-blockers (reduce heart strain)  
Oxygen (if low levels)  
Hospital treatment:  
•
•
Cardiac catheterization: stent or balloon to open blocked artery  
Bypass surgery: reroute blood around the blockage  
Heart Failure:  
Goal: Reduce fluid + help heart work better  
Treatment:  
•
•
•
Diuretics (get rid of fluid buildup)  
ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers (improve heart function)  
Lifestyle changes (low sodium, fluid restriction, meds)  
Cardiac Arrest:  
This is a true emergency!  
Immediate actions:  
•
•
•
CPR (manual chest compressions)  
Defibrillator (AED) for shockable rhythms (VF, VT)  
Epinephrine for asystole (non-shockable rhythm)  
7. Key NCLEX Tips  
Term  
NCLEX Focus  
Heart Attack  
Heart Failure  
Cardiac Arrest  
Know symptoms (chest pain, SOB), ECG, treatment order  
Left vs. right symptoms, daily weight, fluid restrictions, meds  
CPR steps, AED use, identify VF vs. asystole
