變 CORONARY ARTERY DISEASE (CAD)  
– THE REASON BEHIND CABG & PCI  
Coronary artery disease (CAD) is a condition where plaque (fatty deposits)  
builds up in the coronary arteries (the arteries supplying the heart muscle),  
reducing blood flow and oxygen.  
When narrowed arteries cause angina (chest pain) or lead to myocardial  
infarction (MI or heart attack), surgical interventions like CABG or PCI are  
considered.  
 1. PERCUTANEOUS CORONARY  
INTERVENTION (PCI)  
 WHAT IS PCI?  
A minimally invasive procedure to open narrowed coronary arteries.  
Includes:  
• Balloon angioplasty (a balloon is inflated to widen the artery)  
• Stent placement (a mesh tube is inserted to keep the artery open)  
離 NURSING PRIORITIES – PRE/POST PCI  
Before:  
• Informed consent  
• Allergy check: iodine or shellfish (contrast dye)  
• NPO (nothing by mouth) 6–8 hrs before  
After:  
• Monitor puncture site (usually femoral or radial) for bleeding or  
hematoma  
• Keep leg straight and flat if femoral approach  
• Monitor peripheral pulses, color, and temperature of affected limb  
• Monitor for chest pain (may indicate re-occlusion)  
• Encourage fluids to flush out contrast dye  
⚠ COMPLICATIONS  
• Bleeding or hematoma at insertion site  
• Retroperitoneal hemorrhage (back/flank pain, hypotension)  
• Reocclusion or stent thrombosis  
• Allergic reaction to contrast  
易 NCLEX Tip: If patient has sudden chest pain after PCI → suspect acute  
stent thrombosis → call provider!  
贈 2. CORONARY ARTERY BYPASS  
GRAFT (CABG)  
 WHAT IS CABG?  
An open-heart surgery that uses a graft (blood vessel) from another part of  
the body (usually the saphenous vein from the leg or internal mammary  
artery) to bypass blocked coronary arteries.  
Used for:  
• Multiple severe blockages  
• Left main coronary artery blockage  
• Failed PCI or ongoing angina/MI  
 PREOPERATIVE CONSIDERATIONS  
• Educate about surgery and recovery expectations  
• Baseline vitals, labs (CBC, electrolytes, coagulation panel)  
• Informed consent  
• Shave chest/leg site, antiseptic scrub  
喙 POSTOPERATIVE NURSING CARE  
• ICU Monitoring (first 24–48 hrs):  
• Hemodynamic monitoring (arterial line, central line, cardiac output)  
• Chest tubes: monitor drainage (should gradually decrease)  
• Ventilator support: assess readiness for extubation  
• Monitor for:  
◦ Arrhythmias (especially atrial fibrillation)  
◦ Bleeding or cardiac tamponade (muffled heart sounds, JVD,  
hypotension)  
◦ Infection (fever, redness, drainage)  
◦ Electrolyte imbalance (K⁺, Mg²⁺)  
 RECOVERY & TEACHING  
• Early ambulation: prevents DVT and pneumonia  
• Incentive spirometry: prevent atelectasis  
• Pain management  
• Cardiac rehabilitation  
• Low-sodium, low-fat diet, smoking cessation  
易 NCLEX Tip: CABG patients must be taught sternal precautions:  
• No lifting >5–10 lbs for 6–8 weeks  
• No pushing/pulling with arms  
• Hug pillow when coughing

 COMMON QUESTIONS  
• Which intervention for multiple vessel disease? → CABG  
• Post-PCI concern: back pain + hypotension? → Retroperitoneal bleed  
• Complication after CABG: muffled heart sounds? → Tamponade  
• PCI patient with chest pain? → Notify provider, suspect reocclusion
