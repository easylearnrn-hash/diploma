 ENDOCARDITIS – NCLEX  
COMPREHENSIVE OVERVIEW  
✅ WHAT IS ENDOCARDITIS?  
Endocarditis is inflammation of the endocardium, the inner lining of the  
heart, most commonly affecting the heart valves. If untreated, it can lead to:  
• Valve dysfunction (valves can’t open/close properly)  
• Heart failure  
• Systemic infection  
• Embolism (clots traveling to other organs)  
隣 STRUCTURES AFFECTED  
• Heart valves:  
◦ Mitral, Tricuspid, Aortic, Pulmonic  
• Chordae tendineae:  
◦ Fibrous strings connecting valves to muscles inside the heart  
• Interventricular septum:  
◦ Wall that separates the right and left ventricles  
裂 CAUSES OF ENDOCARDITIS  
• Infections (most common):  
◦ Bacteria or fungi in the bloodstream  
• Invasive procedures:  
◦ Dental work, surgery, pacemaker placement, IV lines  
• Heart conditions:  
◦ Congenital defects, valve disease, prosthetic valves  
• IV drug use:  
◦ Injecting unsterile drugs introduces pathogens directly into blood  
溺 TYPES OF ENDOCARDITIS  
1. INFECTIVE ENDOCARDITIS (IE)  
Caused by bacteria, fungi, or viruses attaching to heart tissue and forming  
vegetations (infected clots).  
 RISK FACTORS:  
• IV drug use  
• Artificial heart valves  
• Congenital heart defects  
• Cardiac implants (pacemakers)  
• History of endocarditis  
• Recent dental/surgical procedures  
喝 TYPES OF INFECTIVE ENDOCARDITIS:  
Type  
Features  
Acute Infective  
Endocarditis (AIE)  
Sudden, severe in a healthy valve. High fever, rapid onset.  
Usually Staphylococcus aureus.  
Subacute Infective  
Endocarditis (SIE)  
Slower, milder onset in a patient with pre-existing heart  
disease. Caused by Staphylococcus viridans.  
2. NON-INFECTIVE ENDOCARDITIS  
Not caused by pathogens, but by clots or immune damage.  
 CAUSES:  
• Autoimmune conditions (e.g., Lupus, Antiphospholipid syndrome)  
• Hypercoagulable states (e.g., pregnancy, cancer, DVT)  
• Valve trauma  
⚠ These sterile thrombi can later become infected, leading to infective  
endocarditis.  
 PATHOPHYSIOLOGY – HOW  
INFECTIVE ENDOCARDITIS DEVELOPS  
1. Damage to valve (from trauma or disease)  
2. Platelets + fibrin form a sterile clot (thrombus)  
3. Bacteremia (bacteria enter blood from dental work, IV drugs, etc.)  
4. Bacteria attach to clot and grow → form infected vegetations  
5. Embolization: pieces break off and travel → causing organ damage

⚠ COMPLICATIONS  
• Heart failure (from damaged valves)  
• Septic embolism → Stroke, kidney damage, lung infarction  
• Abscesses in heart, brain, or spleen  
離 DIAGNOSIS  
喝 1. BLOOD CULTURES  
• Identifies the specific organism (3 sets, from separate sites)  
 2. TRANSESOPHAGEAL ECHOCARDIOGRAM  
(TEE)  
• High-detail imaging to detect vegetations on valves  
 3. ECG (ELECTROCARDIOGRAM)  
• Monitors for conduction abnormalities  
 TREATMENT  
• IV Antibiotics:  
◦ Typically for 4–6 weeks (e.g., Vancomycin, Ceftriaxone)  
◦ Adjusted based on culture results  
• Surgery (if severe):  
◦ Valve repair or replacement

識 PATIENT TEACHING  
1. Complete the full antibiotic course  
2. Report signs of infection or embolism  
3. Good oral hygiene – Brush/floss daily, regular dental visits  
4. Prophylactic antibiotics before procedures (if high risk)  
5. Avoid IV drug use  
6. Central line care (if on home IV therapy)  
 NCLEX PRACTICE QUESTION  
EXAMPLE  
Question:  
A client with infective endocarditis reports new onset confusion and left-sided  
weakness. What is the nurse’s priority action?  
A. Document findings and continue monitoring  
B. Notify the healthcare provider immediately  
C. Reassess in 1 hour  
D. Administer anti-seizure medication  
✅ Correct Answer: B  
This could indicate a stroke from embolization—a medical emergency.  
 KEY TAKEAWAYS  
✅ Endocarditis = inflammation of heart’s inner lining, mainly the valves  
✅ IV drug use, prosthetic valves, and heart defects increase risk  
✅ Early symptoms may be vague; fever + new murmur are red flags  
✅ Embolism and heart failure are major risks  
✅ Treated with IV antibiotics and sometimes surgery  
✅ Nurses must monitor for emboli, heart failure, and patient compliance  
✅ Good oral hygiene and prophylactic antibiotics are vital in prevention
