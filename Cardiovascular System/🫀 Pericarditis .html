變 PERICARDITIS OVERVIEW  
Pericarditis is the inflammation (-itis) of the pericardium (the double-layered  
sac surrounding the heart). It can impair cardiac function and lead to serious  
complications like cardiac tamponade.  
 CAUSES OF PERICARDITIS  
• Viral infections (most common, e.g., coxsackievirus)  
• Bacterial infections (e.g., tuberculosis)  
• Post-MI (Myocardial infarction): "Dressler’s Syndrome" (autoimmune  
pericarditis post-heart attack)  
• Autoimmune diseases (e.g., lupus, rheumatoid arthritis)  
• Trauma or injury  
• Uremia (accumulation of urea in blood due to kidney failure)  
• Cancer  
• Radiation therapy  
易 SIGNS & SYMPTOMS – "THE 4 P'S"  
• Pain – sharp, pleuritic (worsens with inspiration), chest pain  
• Pain improves when sitting up and leaning forward  
• Position – sitting up and leaning forward relieves pain  
• Pericardial friction rub – scratchy, grating sound heard on auscultation  
• Pulsus paradoxus – drop in systolic BP >10 mmHg during inspiration  
(seen in tamponade)  
喙 Additional symptoms:  
• Low-grade fever  
• Fatigue  
• Dyspnea (difficulty breathing)  
• Tachycardia (fast heart rate)  
離 DIAGNOSIS  
• EKG (electrocardiogram): Diffuse ST elevation (not localized like in MI),  
PR segment depression  
• Chest X-ray or echocardiogram: Pericardial effusion (fluid around heart)  
• Elevated inflammatory markers: ESR (erythrocyte sedimentation rate),  
CRP (C-reactive protein)  
• Leukocytosis: High WBCs (white blood cells)  
 TREATMENT  
• NSAIDs (Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) – for pain and  
inflammation (e.g., ibuprofen)  
• Colchicine – anti-inflammatory used especially for recurrent pericarditis  
• Corticosteroids – only in severe or refractory cases  
• Antibiotics – only if bacterial infection is confirmed  
• Pericardiocentesis – drainage of fluid if tamponade occurs  
• Monitor for tamponade  
⚠ COMPLICATION: CARDIAC TAMPONADE  
• A life-threatening emergency caused by fluid accumulation compressing  
the heart.  
 Signs: Beck’s Triad  
• Muffled heart sounds  
• Hypotension (low blood pressure)  
• Jugular venous distension (JVD)  
 Other signs:  
• Pulsus paradoxus  
• Narrowed pulse pressure  
• Decreased cardiac output  
易 NCLEX TIPS & PRIORITIES  
• Position patient upright and leaning forward  
• Monitor for cardiac tamponade signs (Beck’s triad, pulsus paradoxus)  
• Administer NSAIDs and monitor pain response  
• Monitor vitals and ECG changes  
• Educate on activity restrictions during acute phase  
• Avoid anticoagulants if at risk for tamponade
