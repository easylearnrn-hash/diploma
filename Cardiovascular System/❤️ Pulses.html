❤ PULSES  
喙 WHAT IS A PULSE?  
A pulse is the palpable rhythmic expansion of an artery caused by the  
contraction of the heart (specifically, left ventricular systole — the phase  
where the heart contracts and pumps blood). It reflects heart rate (beats per  
minute), rhythm, and strength (amplitude).  
吝 COMMON PULSE SITES AND THEIR MEANINGS  
1. APICAL PULSE  
• Location: 5th intercostal space at the midclavicular line (between ribs,  
near the nipple line on the left side of the chest).  
• Use: Most accurate pulse. Always used in infants (<2 years), cardiac  
meds (e.g., digoxin), and irregular rhythms.  
• Auscultated (listened to) with a stethoscope, not palpated.  
• Count for 1 full minute if irregular or giving cardiac meds.  
2. RADIAL PULSE  
• Location: Thumb side of the wrist (radial artery).  
• Use: Most commonly palpated pulse in adults.  
• Assess for: Rate, rhythm, strength.  
3. BRACHIAL PULSE  
• Location: Inside upper arm, near elbow crease (antecubital fossa).  
• Use: Best pulse for checking circulation in infants. Also used in BP  
measurement.  
4. CAROTID PULSE  
• Location: Side of the neck beside the trachea (carotid artery).  
• Use: Emergency pulse check in unconscious adults (e.g., CPR).  
• Warning: Never palpate both carotids simultaneously → causes vagal  
stimulation (↓ HR, syncope).  
5. FEMORAL PULSE  
• Location: Groin area (inguinal region).  
• Use: Assess circulation to the lower extremities, especially in trauma or  
shock.  
6. POPLITEAL PULSE  
• Location: Behind the knee.  
• Use: Difficult to find but essential to evaluate lower leg circulation.  
7. POSTERIOR TIBIAL PULSE  
• Location: Inner ankle (behind the medial malleolus).  
• Use: Peripheral circulation check in lower limbs.  
8. DORSALIS PEDIS (PEDAL) PULSE  
• Location: Top of the foot (dorsum).  
• Use: Crucial for assessing peripheral perfusion (especially in diabetes,  
peripheral vascular disease, and post-op limb surgery).  
 PULSE CHARACTERISTICS TO ASSESS  
 RATE  
• Normal: 60–100 bpm (beats per minute) in adults  
• Bradycardia (<60 bpm), Tachycardia (>100 bpm)  
• Use apical pulse when irregular or on cardiac meds  
 RHYTHM  
• Regular vs. Irregular  
• Irregularly irregular rhythms suggest atrial fibrillation  
• Compare apical-radial pulse (if difference = pulse deficit, may indicate  
heart failure or dysrhythmia)  
 AMPLITUDE (STRENGTH)  
• 0 = Absent  
• 1+ = Weak, thready  
• 2+ = Normal  
• 3+ = Strong  
• 4+ = Bounding (seen in fever, anxiety, anemia)  
 EQUALITY  
• Compare bilaterally (e.g., both radial or both pedal pulses)  
• Asymmetry may suggest vascular obstruction or impaired perfusion  
易 NCLEX RN PRIORITY CONCEPTS  
 ALWAYS ASSESS APICAL PULSE FOR:  
• Cardiac medications like digoxin, beta-blockers  
• Newborns and infants  
• Irregular or weak peripheral pulses  
 USE PEDAL PULSES TO:  
• Evaluate circulatory status in diabetics  
• Assess compartment syndrome, deep vein thrombosis (DVT), peripheral  
arterial disease (PAD)  
 SUSPECT IMPAIRED PERFUSION IF:  
• Pulses are diminished or absent  
• Extremities are pale, cool, or cyanotic (bluish discoloration)  
• Capillary refill time > 2 seconds  
• Use Doppler if pulses not palpable (ultrasound device if pulses are not  
palpable (cannot be felt by hand)  
 PULSE DEFICIT  
• When apical pulse > radial pulse  
• Indicates inefficient cardiac contractions (e.g., atrial fibrillation)  
 EMERGENCY PULSE USE  
• Carotid: Use during CPR on adults  
• Brachial: Best for infants in CPR  
• Femoral: Check in trauma, shock, hemorrhage  
 DOCUMENTATION EXAMPLE  
"Radial pulse 82 bpm, regular rhythm, 2+ amplitude, equal  
bilaterally"  
離 RELATED LAB/ASSESSMENT CORRELATIONS  
Bradycardia + digoxin → Hold med if apical pulse <60 bpm  
Bounding pulses → Associated with anemia, fever, anxiety, hyperthyroidism  
Thready/weak pulse → Seen in dehydration, hypovolemia, shock
