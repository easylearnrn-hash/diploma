喝 BLOOD, BLOOD VESSELS, & LYMPH  
NODES – NCLEX OVERVIEW  
喝 BLOOD BASICS  
Blood = Liquid connective tissue made up of plasma (fluid portion) and  
formed elements (cells).  
✨ BLOOD COMPONENTS  
• Plasma (fluid part of blood) – 55% of blood volume; carries nutrients,  
hormones, waste products.  
• Red Blood Cells (RBCs) (erythrocytes) – Transport oxygen (O₂) via  
hemoglobin.  
• White Blood Cells (WBCs) (leukocytes) – Defend the body against  
infection.  
• Platelets (thrombocytes) – Help blood clot to prevent bleeding.  
1. RED BLOOD CELLS (ERYTHROCYTES)  
• Job: Carry oxygen (O₂) to tissues, bring CO₂ back to lungs.  
• Hemoglobin (protein inside RBCs) binds oxygen.  
• Iron (Fe) is critical for hemoglobin's oxygen-carrying ability.  
• Anemia = Low RBCs or hemoglobin → Fatigue, pallor, SOB (shortness of  
breath).  
Fact:  
• RBCs give blood its red color.  
• RBCs live about 120 days.  
2. WHITE BLOOD CELLS (LEUKOCYTES)  
• Job: Fight infections, destroy pathogens.  
Types: (Never Let Monkeys Eat Bananas)  
• Neutrophils: Bacteria killers  
• Lymphocytes: Viral fighters (B cells produce antibodies, T cells kill  
infected cells)  
• Monocytes, Eosinophils, Basophils: Specialized immune responses  
(parasites, allergies)  
Leukopenia = Low WBCs → Risk of infection.  
3. PLATELETS (THROMBOCYTES)  
Job: Form clots to stop bleeding.  
How:  
• Stick together at the injury site (platelet plug).  
• Activate clotting factors → fibrin mesh seals wound.  
Thrombocytopenia = Low platelets → Easy bruising, bleeding.  
4. PLASMA  
Job: Fluid carrying blood cells and substances.  
Contains:  
• Water (90%)  
• Nutrients (glucose, amino acids, vitamins)  
• Hormones (chemical messengers)  
• Waste products (to kidneys, liver)  
• Proteins (albumin for fluid balance, clotting factors for coagulation)  
 WHY BLOOD IS ESSENTIAL  
 Delivers oxygen and nutrients.  
 Removes waste (CO₂, urea).  
 Fights infections (WBCs).  
啕 Prevents bleeding (platelets + clotting proteins).

 BLOOD VESSELS  
• Arteries (vessels carrying blood away from the heart)  
→ High-pressure, oxygen-rich (except pulmonary artery which is oxygen-  
poor)  
• Veins (vessels carrying blood toward the heart)  
→ Low-pressure, oxygen-poor (except pulmonary vein which is oxygen-  
rich)  
• Capillaries (tiny vessels where gas, nutrient, and waste exchange occurs  
between blood and tissues)

 HEART-RELATED CONNECTIONS  
Aorta (largest artery) – Carries oxygenated blood from heart to body.  
Vena Cava (largest vein) – Returns deoxygenated blood from body to heart.  
Pulmonary Artery – Carries deoxygenated blood from heart to lungs.  
Pulmonary Vein – Carries oxygenated blood from lungs to heart.  
易 KEY BLOOD TERMS FOR NCLEX  
Hematopoiesis (production of blood cells) → Happens in bone marrow.  
Anemia (low RBCs or low hemoglobin) → Causes fatigue, pallor, weakness.  
Polycythemia (too many RBCs) → Risk of blood clots.  
Thrombocytopenia (low platelets) → Risk of bleeding.  
Leukopenia (low WBCs) → Risk of infection.

易 COAGULATION CASCADE (BLOOD  
CLOTTING)  
Involves platelets + clotting factors (proteins that work in sequence to form a  
clot).  
Fibrin (protein mesh) forms the final clot.  
Key Labs:  
• PT (Prothrombin Time) – Measures clotting via extrinsic pathway (used  
for Warfarin monitoring).  
• INR (International Normalized Ratio) – Standardized PT value (target 2–  
3 for anticoagulation).  
• aPTT (Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time) – Measures clotting via  
intrinsic pathway (used for Heparin monitoring).  
喙 BLOOD PRESSURE EXPLAINED  
• Measured in arteries, not veins.  
• Normal BP:  
◦ Systolic (<120 mmHg): Heart contracts.  
◦ Diastolic (<80 mmHg): Heart relaxes.  
• Highest Pressure: In arteries (especially aorta).  
• Lowest Pressure: In veins near heart.  
Hypertension = High blood pressure (>130/80 mmHg) → Stroke, MI risk.  
Hypotension = Low blood pressure → Fainting, poor circulation.  
 LYMPHATIC SYSTEM – THE  
CLEANUP CREW  
• Lymphatic System = Network that drains fluid from tissues back into the  
bloodstream.  
• Lymph (fluid containing WBCs) – Flows through lymphatic vessels.  
• Lymph Nodes (small bean-shaped organs) – Filter lymph, trap  
pathogens, and activate immune response.  
WHAT THE LYMPHATIC SYSTEM DOES:  
• Prevents swelling (edema)  
◦ Collects fluid leaking from capillaries and returns it to blood.  
• Fights infections  
◦ Lymph nodes scan lymph for pathogens.  
• Absorbs fats  
◦ Special vessels called lacteals absorb fats from intestines.

 NCLEX TIP: COMMON LYMPH NODE  
FINDINGS  
Painless, hard lymph node → Possible cancer (e.g., lymphoma).  
Painful, tender lymph node → Infection/inflammation.  
Neck, armpits, and groin are the common places to palpate (feel) lymph  
nodes.
