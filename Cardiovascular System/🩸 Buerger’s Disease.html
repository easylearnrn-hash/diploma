喝 BUERGER’S DISEASE  
(THROMBOANGIITIS OBLITERANS)  
 DEFINITION  
Buerger’s Disease is a non-atherosclerotic (not due to cholesterol plaque  
buildup), segmental (affecting discrete portions) inflammatory disease of  
the small and medium arteries and veins of the arms and legs. It leads to  
blood vessel inflammation (vasculitis), clotting (thrombosis), and eventual  
blockage (occlusion) of circulation.  
離 ETIOLOGY (CAUSE)  
• Unknown exact cause, but strongly associated with tobacco use,  
especially in young men under 45.  
• Can occur with smoking, vaping, or chewing tobacco.  
• Possibly autoimmune or hypersensitivity to tobacco.  
⚠ RISK FACTORS  
• Male sex, age < 45 years  
• Tobacco use (nicotine exposure)  
• Chronic periodontal infection  
• Family history  
• Living in cold climates (can aggravate symptoms)  
溺 PATHOPHYSIOLOGY  
• Tobacco triggers inflammation (immune response) in the vascular  
endothelium (inner lining of vessels).  
• Inflammation leads to thrombosis (clot formation) and fibrosis  
(scarring).  
• Blood flow is obstructed, causing ischemia (lack of oxygen) in fingers  
and toes.  
• Can lead to ulcers, gangrene (tissue death), and amputation if not  
treated.  
易 SIGNS & SYMPTOMS (NCLEX MNEMONIC:  
“BURGER”)  
• B: Burning pain in hands/feet (rest pain)  
• U: Ulcers on digits (painful, non-healing) (A sore or open wound that  
develops on the fingers or toes)  
• R: Redness or cyanosis (bluish color) when limb is down  
• G: Gangrene (black, necrotic areas)  
• E: Extremity cool and pale on elevation  
• R: Reduced/absent pulses in affected limbs  
 DIAGNOSIS  
• Physical exam: Symptoms, reduced pulses, skin changes  
• Doppler ultrasound: Checks for blood flow  
• Angiography: Visualizes blocked arteries  
• Allen’s Test: Assesses radial/ulnar artery patency  
• Rule out other causes (atherosclerosis, embolism, diabetes)  
✅ STEPS OF THE ALLEN'S TEST:  
• Patient clenches fist to force blood out of the hand.  
• The nurse occludes both the radial and ulnar arteries using firm  
pressure.  
• Patient then opens hand — the palm will appear pale due to lack of  
blood flow.  
• The nurse then releases pressure on the ulnar artery while keeping the  
radial artery compressed.  
• Observe:  
◦ If the hand flushes (reddens) within 5–7 seconds, the ulnar artery  
is patent (open) — test is positive (normal).  
◦ If no color returns, the ulnar artery is not providing adequate  
blood flow — test is negative (abnormal).  
 TREATMENT GOALS  
Main goals: Prevent progression, relieve symptoms, avoid  
amputation  
璘 PRIMARY INTERVENTION  
•  Absolute smoking cessation – most important!  
NCLEX TIP: Even nicotine patches/gum can worsen disease  
 OTHER TREATMENTS  
• Vasodilators (meds to widen blood vessels)  
• Antiplatelets (prevent clotting)  
• Pain control  
• Wound care for ulcers  
• Amputation (if gangrene develops)  
❄ NON-PHARMACOLOGIC  
• Warmth to extremities (avoid cold exposure)  
• Avoid trauma to feet/hands  
• Exercise programs (promotes circulation)  
• Stress management  
 NURSING INTERVENTIONS  
• Assess circulation (color, temp, pulses)  
• Encourage smoking cessation support programs  
• Provide pain relief  
• Monitor for infection and signs of necrosis  
• Teach foot care: daily inspection, clean/dry, soft footwear  
• Educate on medication adherence  
❗ NCLEX KEY POINTS  
• Most important first action? Ask about tobacco use.  
• Top priority intervention? Smoking cessation.  
• Expected symptoms? Pain at rest, cold/pale digits, ulcerations.  
• What to avoid? Cold, trauma, nicotine patches.  
• Complication to monitor? Gangrene → amputation.
