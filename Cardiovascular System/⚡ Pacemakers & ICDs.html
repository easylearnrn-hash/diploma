⚡ PACEMAKERS / ICDS (IMPLANTABLE  
CARDIOVERTER DEFIBRILLATORS)  
 PACEMAKERS  
A pacemaker is a device implanted under the skin that delivers electrical  
impulses to stimulate the heart to contract and maintain an adequate heart  
rate.  
• Temporary: External wires (used post-MI or during acute bradycardia)  
• Permanent: Implanted surgically  
 INDICATIONS:  
• Bradycardia (slow heart rate)  
• Heart block (electrical signals blocked between atria and ventricles)  
• Asystole (flatline)  
 NURSING PRIORITIES:  
• Monitor ECG to verify capture (pacing spike followed by QRS)  
• Avoid lifting arm on affected side for 1–2 weeks  
• No MRI unless pacer is MRI-compatible  
• Educate patient to report dizziness, hiccups (may indicate lead  
displacement)  
• Avoid strong magnets (airport security, headphones over chest)  
易 NCLEX Tip: If you see pacer spikes not followed by QRS → suspect loss of  
capture.  
⚡ ICD (IMPLANTABLE CARDIOVERTER  
DEFIBRILLATOR)  
An ICD detects and shocks the heart during life-threatening arrhythmias (like  
ventricular tachycardia or fibrillation).  
• Often combined with pacemaker  
• Instruct patient: If ICD fires, lie down and notify provider if symptoms  
continue  
❤ NURSING PRIORITIES FOR CHEST  
PAIN  
When a patient complains of chest pain, follow the ABC approach and  
remember MONA (Morphine, Oxygen, Nitrates, Aspirin – not in  
administration order):  
✅ PRIORITY ACTIONS (ABC-FOCUSED):  
1. Assess ABCs  
2. Apply oxygen (if O2 sat <90%)  
3. Vital signs  
4. 12-lead ECG within 10 minutes  
5. IV access  
6. Labs: Troponin, CK-MB  
7. Administer meds:  
• Aspirin (antiplatelet)  
• Nitroglycerin (vasodilator)  
• Morphine (if pain not relieved)  
8. Chest X-ray (rule out other causes)  
易 NCLEX Tip: Never delay ECG in chest pain. Pain radiating to jaw/left arm in  
women/elderly may be MI.

綾 PATIENT EDUCATION  
⚠ DIET:  
• Low-sodium (less than 2g/day)  
• Low-saturated fat, low-cholesterol  
• Increase fiber, fruits/veggies  
• Avoid processed foods, deli meats, canned soups  
 FLUID RESTRICTION:  
• Especially in heart failure or renal disease  
• Limit to 1.5–2 L/day (monitor intake/output)  
 LIFESTYLE MODIFICATIONS:  
No smoking  
• Weight loss if BMI >25  
• Moderate physical activity (150 min/week)  
• Manage stress  
• Blood pressure & glucose control  
易 NCLEX PRIORITY EDUCATION:  
• Daily weights (report gain >2 lbs/day or 5 lbs/week)  
• Adhere to medications: ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, diuretics  
• Get flu/pneumonia vaccines  
• Monitor for signs of decompensation: SOB, swelling, chest pain, fatigue
