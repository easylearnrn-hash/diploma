1. WHAT IS SHOCK?  
• Shock is a life-threatening condition where the body’s organs do not  
get enough oxygen and nutrients due to poor blood circulation.  
• If untreated, shock can lead to organ failure and death.  
 NCLEX Tip: Shock = Decreased Blood Flow → Low Oxygen → Organ  
Failure!

3. GENERAL SIGNS & SYMPTOMS OF  
SHOCK  
 Early Signs (Compensated Shock – Body Trying to Adjust)  
❌ Tachycardia (Fast Heart Rate – Trying to Maintain Blood Flow).  
❌ Hypotension (Low Blood Pressure – Due to Poor Circulation).  
❌ Cool, Clammy Skin (Blood is Sent to Vital Organs Instead of the Skin).  
❌ Restlessness & Anxiety (Low Oxygen to the Brain).  
 Late Signs (Decompensated Shock – Organs Failing)  
❌ Severe Hypotension (Dangerously Low BP).  
❌ Weak or Absent Peripheral Pulses (No Blood Reaching Extremities).  
❌ Slow, Shallow Breathing (Respiratory Failure).  
❌ Loss of Consciousness (Brain Not Getting Enough Oxygen).  
 NCLEX Tip: Shock starts with high heart rate (compensation) but later  
leads to low BP & organ failure if untreated!  
4. SHOCK MANAGEMENT –  
EMERGENCY CARE ("ABCS FIRST!")  
✅ A – Airway  
• Ensure the airway is open and clear of obstructions.  
• Intubation (Breathing Tube) may be needed if the patient cannot  
breathe properly.  
✅ B – Breathing  
• Provide oxygen (100% via non-rebreather mask or ventilator if  
needed).  
• Monitor respiratory rate and effort.  
✅ C – Circulation  
• IV Fluids (To Restore Blood Volume – Especially in Hypovolemic &  
Septic Shock).  
• Vasopressors (Medications That Raise Blood Pressure – Used in  
Distributive Shock).  
• Blood Transfusion (If Blood Loss Is Severe – Used in Hemorrhagic  
Shock).  
 NCLEX Tip: Shock = Give Oxygen, Fluids, and Vasopressors (If BP is  
dangerously low)!

6. MONITORING & NURSING  
INTERVENTIONS FOR SHOCK  
✅ Frequent Vital Signs (Monitor BP, HR, RR Closely).  
✅ Monitor Urine Output (Goal: ≥30 mL/hr – Low Output Means Kidneys  
Are Failing!).  
✅ Assess Level of Consciousness (Confusion & Drowsiness = Worsening  
Shock).  
✅ Trendelenburg Position (Legs Elevated) MAY be used for Hypovolemic  
Shock (To Increase Blood Flow to the Heart).  
✅ Monitor for Signs of Organ Failure (Cold Extremities, No Urine Output,  
Confusion, Respiratory Failure).  
 NCLEX Tip: Low urine output = Kidneys not receiving enough blood →  
Organ failure!  
7. NCLEX "MUST-KNOW" SHOCK  
EMERGENCY CARE TIPS  
 Shock = Low Oxygen & Poor Blood Flow → Life-Threatening If  
Untreated!  
 First Action in Shock = Oxygen + IV Fluids!  
 Septic Shock = Give IV Antibiotics ASAP!  
 Hypovolemic Shock = Give Fluids First (Then Blood If Needed)!  
 Cardiogenic Shock = DO NOT Give Too Many Fluids (Heart Can’t Handle  
It)!  
 Anaphylactic Shock = Epinephrine First!  
 BP Too Low? Give Vasopressors (Norepinephrine, Dopamine)!  
 No Urine Output = Organ Failure → Emergency!
