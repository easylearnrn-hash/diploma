 IV GAUGES AND THEIR USES  
 WHAT IS A GAUGE?  
The gauge refers to the diameter of the needle or catheter.  
Smaller number = Larger needle (e.g., 14G is larger than 24G).

喝 BLOOD TRANSFUSION BASICS  
✅ INDICATIONS FOR BLOOD TRANSFUSION  
• Anemia (low red blood cells or hemoglobin)  
• Hemorrhage (active bleeding)  
• Clotting disorders  
• Surgery or trauma with blood loss  
• Thrombocytopenia (low platelets)  
離 TYPES OF BLOOD PRODUCTS  
• Packed Red Blood Cells (PRBCs) – for anemia or blood loss  
• Platelets – for thrombocytopenia or active bleeding  
• Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP) – for clotting factor replacement  
(coagulopathy, liver failure)  
• Cryoprecipitate – high in fibrinogen and factor VIII  
• Albumin – for volume expansion, burns, or hypoalbuminemia  
立 PRE-TRANSFUSION NURSING  
RESPONSIBILITIES  
• Verify order – match the correct patient, blood type, and product.  
• Informed consent – ensure the patient or legal guardian has signed.  
• Check baseline vital signs – especially temperature.  
• Blood compatibility check (crossmatch) – confirm ABO & Rh.  
• Use appropriate IV gauge – 18G or 20G preferred for PRBCs.  
• Use blood tubing with a filter – NOT regular IV tubing.  
• Prime tubing with 0.9% normal saline (NS) – only NS, no dextrose or LR!  
⏳ DURING TRANSFUSION  
• Start slowly: 2 mL/min for the first 15 minutes.  
• Stay with the patient for the first 15 minutes – this is the most critical  
time for a reaction.  
• Reassess vitals at:  
◦ 15 minutes, then  
◦ Every 30 minutes  
• After completion  
◦ Infuse within 4 hours of removal from refrigeration to prevent  
bacterial growth.

⚠ SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS  
• Never add medications to blood products.  
• Blood should be started within 30 minutes of release from the blood  
bank.  
• Blood tubing should be changed every 4 hours or after 2 units.  
• Document: start/end time, vitals, patient tolerance, and any reaction.  
啕 PEDIATRIC & ELDERLY  
CONSIDERATIONS  
• May require smaller gauge (22G or 24G).  
• Slower infusion rates.  
• More frequent monitoring due to risk of fluid overload.  
 NCLEX HIGH-YIELD FACTS  
• Best needle size for PRBCs: 18G  
• Only compatible fluid: 0.9% normal saline (NS)  
• Start slow and monitor closely for the first 15 minutes.  
• Stop transfusion immediately if reaction suspected.  
• Always double-check patient ID and blood unit with 2 licensed  
personnel.
