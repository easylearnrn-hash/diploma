PVD (Peripheral Venous Disease)  
Treatment  
Condition/Cause  
Monitor  
Peripheral Venous Disease (PVD) is a condition  
affecting the veins, where the blood flow from  
the lower extremities back to the heart is  
impaired.  
Elevate Legs:  
Complications  
● Advise the patient to elevate their legs when  
sitting to promote better blood flow back to the  
heart.  
Valve Problems: The veins have valves that help  
direct blood flow upwards. Dysfunctional valves  
can lead to PVD.  
Note  
Thrombus: Blood clots in the veins can obstruct  
blood flow.  
Atherosclerosis: Hardening and narrowing of  
the veins due to plaque buildup.  
Right-Sided Heart Failure: The heart's inability  
to effectively pump blood from the veins back to  
the heart can lead to PVD.  
Symptoms  
Peripheral Edema: In the lower extremities.  
Pitting Edema: More than 2 seconds after  
pressing on the swollen area.  
Skin Changes: Yellow and brown legs and  
ankles. Thick, tough skin.  
Temperature: Legs are warm to the touch  
Ulcers: Irregularly shaped ulcers on the legs,  
typically not very painful.  
Peripheral Pulse: Bounding pulse (+3) in the  
affected areas.  
Pain: Weak dull pain or no pain

PAD (Peripheral Artery Disease)  
Treatment  
Condition/Cause  
Monitor  
Peripheral Artery Disease (PAD) is a condition  
where the arteries that supply blood to the lower  
extremities become narrowed or blocked, leading  
to reduced blood flow and oxygen to the tissues.  
Arteries do not have valves because they rely on  
the heart's pressure to circulate blood. Gravity  
does not significantly affect arterial blood flow.  
Leg Position:  
Complications  
Note  
● Advise the patient to keep their legs down to  
improve blood flow.  
Foot Care:  
● Proper care of the feet to prevent ulcers and  
infections due to poor circulation.  
Note  
Symptoms  
Very Sharp Pain: Due to reduced blood flow  
and oxygen  
Cold to Touch: Legs feel cold to touch due to  
poor circulation.  
Cyanosis: Blue color of the skin indicating  
lack of oxygen.  
Weak Peripheral Pulse: Pulse may be weak  
(+1) or absent (0).  
Ulcers: Round-shaped ulcers may develop due  
to poor blood supply.  
Hair Loss: Loss of hair on the legs, Thin,  
dry/scaly skin due to inadequate circulation.  
Nail Changes: Nails may become brittle  
(thick), cracked, or fall off due to lack of blood  
flow.

DVT (Deep Venous Thrombosis)  
Treatment  
Condition/Cause  
Monitor  
Coagulation Problems: Increased blood clot  
formation.  
Anticoagulants:  
1. Regular measurement of the calf  
● Do NOT dissolve an existing clot but help prevent  
the clot from enlarging and new clots from  
forming.  
2. Bleeding risk Thrombolytics  
Prolonged Immobilization: Extended periods of  
inactivity can lead to clot formation.  
Complications  
Thrombolytics:  
Pulmonary Embolism (PE): A  
serious condition where a blood clot  
breaks free, travels through the vena  
cava to the right heart, and then to the  
lungs, causing a blockage.  
Symptoms  
● Catheter-directed clot-busting agents, such as  
tissue Plasminogen Activator (tPA). Or IVC filter  
Compression Devices:  
Sharp Calf Pain: Pain localized to the calf  
muscle.  
● Use of compressive devices on the legs to  
improve blood flow and prevent clot formation  
during periods of immobilization.  
Unilateral Edema: Swelling in one leg  
Visible Thrombus: Swollen vein may appear  
prominent and be warm to the touch.  
Pain and Tenderness: Pain at the site of the  
thrombus, especially in the calf.  
Virchow's Triad  
● Stasis (Impairment) of Venous Circulation  
● Hypercoagulability  
● Endothelial Damage (Damage to the Lining)  
Homan's Sign - test used to diagnose DVT  
1. The patient lies down with the leg straight.  
2. The healthcare provider dorsiflexes the patient's  
foot (bends it upward toward the shin).  
3. Pain in the calf during this maneuver is  
considered a positive Homan's sign.  
A positive Homan's sign may suggest the presence of a  
deep vein thrombosis (DVT), but it requires confirmation  
with additional tests like an ultrasound.
