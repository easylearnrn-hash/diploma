Prioritization of Organs  
The body prioritizes blood flow and resources to organs based on their critical functions. Ordered by priority:  
● Brain: Essential for consciousness, cognition, and overall control of bodily functions.  
● Heart: Crucial for pumping blood throughout the body, maintaining circulation.  
● Lungs: Vital for gas exchange, oxygenating blood, and removing carbon dioxide.  
● Liver: Key for detoxification, metabolism, and production of essential proteins and biochemicals.  
● Kidneys: Important for blood filtration, waste removal and regulating fluid and electrolyte balance.  
● Pancreas: Produces insulin and other hormones for glucose regulation and digestive enzymes.  
● Gastrointestinal Tract: Includes the stomach, intestines, and other digestive organs. Important for nutrient  
absorption and digestion.  
● Spleen: Functions in blood filtration, immune response, and recycling of red blood cells.  
● Adrenal Glands: Produce hormones such as adrenaline and cortisol, which are crucial for stress response and  
metabolic processes.  
● Bone Marrow: Produces blood cells, including red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets.  
● Muscles: Important for movement, posture, and generating heat.  
● Skin: Acts as a protective barrier, regulates temperature, and provides sensory information.  
● Reproductive Organs: Involved in reproduction and hormone production.  
● Eyes:Essential for vision and receiving visual information.  
● Peripheral Nervous System: Transmits signals between the central nervous system and the rest of the body.  
This prioritization ensures that the most critical organs receive sufficient blood flow and resources to maintain  
essential functions, especially during times of stress or compromised health.
