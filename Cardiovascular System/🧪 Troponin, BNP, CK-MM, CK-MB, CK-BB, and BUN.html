變 CARDIAC & MUSCLE BIOMARKERS  
 1. TROPONIN  
Definition: Troponin is a protein found in heart muscle fibers that is released  
into the blood when there is heart damage, especially from a myocardial  
infarction (MI) (heart attack).  
Troponin I Normal Range: <0.04 ng/mL •  
•
Troponin T Normal Range: <0.01 ng/mL  
•
Onset: Rises within 3–6 hours after injury •  
Peak: 12–24 hours  
•
Returns to Normal: 7–14 days  
✅ NCLEX TIP:  
Troponin is the most specific and sensitive marker for myocardial injury.  
Elevated levels = cardiac damage (e.g., heart attack).  
 2. BNP (B-TYPE NATRIURETIC PEPTIDE)  
Definition: BNP is a hormone released by the ventricles of the heart in  
response to increased pressure and volume overload (as seen in heart  
failure).  
• Normal Range: <100 pg/mL  
• Mild elevation: 100–300 pg/mL (suggests early HF)  
• Moderate to severe: >300 pg/mL (strongly suggests heart failure)  
✅ NCLEX TIP:  
BNP helps differentiate shortness of breath (dyspnea) caused by heart failure  
vs lung conditions (e.g., pneumonia, COPD).  
 Dyspnea = difficulty breathing  
 Heart failure (HF) = when the heart can't pump blood effectively  
 Volume overload = too much fluid in the blood or tissues  
 3. CK (CREATINE KINASE)  
Definition: CK is an enzyme found in the heart, brain, and skeletal muscles  
that’s released when there is tissue damage.  
A. CK-MM (MUSCLE TYPE)  
• Location: Skeletal muscle  
• Increased In: Rhabdomyolysis (muscle breakdown), muscle injury,  
seizures, crushing injuries  
• Normal Range: 96–100% of total CK  
 Rhabdomyolysis = rapid breakdown of muscle tissue → releases myoglobin  
→ can cause kidney damage  
B. CK-MB (CARDIAC TYPE)  
• Location: Heart muscle  
• Increased In: MI (myocardial infarction), myocarditis, cardiac trauma  
• Normal Range: 0–5% of total CK  
• Onset: 4–6 hours  
• Peak: 18–24 hours  
• Returns to Normal: 2–3 days  
✅ NCLEX TIP:  
CK-MB helps detect reinfarction (a second heart attack) since it returns to  
normal faster than troponin.  
C. CK-BB (BRAIN TYPE)  
• Location: Brain tissue  
• Increased In: Brain injury, CVA (stroke), seizures, CNS tumors  
• Normal Range: 0% (should not be detectable)  
 CVA (Cerebrovascular Accident) = stroke  
 CNS = Central Nervous System  
 RENAL (KIDNEY) FUNCTION LAB  
 BUN (BLOOD UREA NITROGEN)  
Definition: BUN measures the amount of urea nitrogen (a waste product  
from protein metabolism) in the blood. It helps assess kidney function and  
hydration status.  
• Normal Range: 7–20 mg/dL  
• Increased BUN: Kidney failure, dehydration, GI bleeding, high protein  
intake  
• Decreased BUN: Malnutrition, liver dysfunction, fluid overload  
✅ NCLEX TIP:  
• Always assess BUN with creatinine to evaluate renal function accurately.  
• Elevated BUN with normal creatinine = dehydration  
• Elevated BUN + elevated creatinine = renal impairment  
 Azotemia = high levels of nitrogen-containing compounds in the blood  
 Uremia = toxic accumulation of urea in blood due to kidney failure

 BONUS: RELATED TERMS YOU MAY  
SEE IN NCLEX  
• Atelectasis: (Collapsed lung or alveoli) → decreased oxygenation  
• Ischemia: (Insufficient blood flow to tissue) → can lead to tissue death  
• Infarction: (Tissue death due to lack of oxygen) → MI = heart tissue  
death  
• Myopathy: (Disease or damage of muscle tissue)  
• Hypoperfusion: (Inadequate blood flow to organs or tissues)  
✅ NCLEX TIPS TO REMEMBER  
• Troponin = gold standard for heart attack.  
• BNP differentiates heart vs lung causes of dyspnea.  
• CK-MB detects reinfarction; returns to normal faster than troponin.  
• Elevated CK-MM? Think muscle damage like rhabdomyolysis.  
• CK-BB rise? Assess for brain injury, stroke, or tumor.  
• BUN high + creatinine high = renal failure.  
• Always monitor I&O (Intake and Output), daily weights, and electrolyte  
levels with kidney issues.
