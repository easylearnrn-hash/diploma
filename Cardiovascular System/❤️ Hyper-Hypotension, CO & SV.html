❤ HYPERTENSION, HYPOTENSION,  
CARDIAC OUTPUT & STROKE  
VOLUME  
 KEY DEFINITIONS  
• Blood Pressure (BP) → The force of blood against the artery walls.  
Formula:  
BP = Cardiac Output × Systemic Vascular Resistance (SVR)  
• Cardiac Output (CO) → The amount of blood pumped by the left  
ventricle in 1 minute.  
Formula:  
CO = Stroke Volume × Heart Rate  
• Stroke Volume (SV) → The amount of blood pumped from the left  
ventricle per beat.  
Normal: ~60–100 mL/beat.  
• Systemic Vascular Resistance (SVR) → The resistance the heart must  
pump against.  
喙 HYPERTENSION (HTN)  
 DEFINITION:  
• Systolic BP ≥ 130 mmHg OR Diastolic BP ≥ 80 mmHg (based on  
current AHA guidelines)  
• Persistent elevation over time, not just one reading.  
 TYPES:  
Type

No identifiable cause (90–95% of cases)

⚠ RISK FACTORS:  
• Non-modifiable: Age, family history, race (higher in African Americans)  
• Modifiable: Obesity, smoking, high sodium diet, sedentary lifestyle,  
alcohol excess, stress  
易 CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS:  
• Often asymptomatic (“silent killer”)  
• Possible headache, dizziness, vision changes, chest pain if severe  
離 COMPLICATIONS:  
• Stroke (CVA), myocardial infarction (MI), heart failure (HF), chronic kidney  
disease (CKD), retinal damage  
 MANAGEMENT:  
• Lifestyle changes: DASH diet, exercise, sodium restriction, stress control  
• Medications: Diuretics, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, Beta-blockers, Calcium  
channel blockers  
喙 HYPOTENSION  
 DEFINITION:  
• Systolic BP < 90 mmHg OR Diastolic BP < 60 mmHg  
• Clinically significant if it causes symptoms or organ hypoperfusion  
 TYPES:  
Type  
Description

Severe hypotension with inadequate tissue perfusion  
易 CAUSES:  
• Hypovolemia (bleeding, dehydration)  
• Heart failure  
• Sepsis  
• Medications (antihypertensives, diuretics)  
• Endocrine disorders (Addison’s disease)  
⚠ SYMPTOMS:  
• Dizziness  
• Fainting (syncope)  
• Blurred vision  
• Confusion  
• Tachycardia  
• Pale/cool skin  
• Low urine output  
 MANAGEMENT:  
• Address underlying cause  
• IV fluids or blood products for hypovolemia  
• Vasopressors (norepinephrine, dopamine) in shock  
• Safety precautions (slow position changes, fall prevention)

NCLEX NOTE: ADMINISTER FLUIDS  
 NCLEX Tip:  
• Hypotension is clinically significant when symptomatic (dizziness,  
syncope, confusion, oliguria).  
• Causes include dehydration, blood loss, sepsis, anaphylaxis, cardiac  
failure, and medication effects.

變 CARDIAC OUTPUT (CO)  
 FORMULA:  
• CO = Stroke Volume × Heart Rate  
• Normal CO: 4–8 L/min  
 FACTORS AFFECTING CO:  
Factor  
Effect  
Preload (ventricular filling)  
Afterload (resistance to pumping)  
Contractility (strength of contraction)  
↑ Preload ↑ SV (to a point)  
↑ Afterload ↓ SV  
↑ Contractility ↑ SV  
⚠ NCLEX CLUES:  
• Low CO → hypotension, weak pulses, cool skin, low urine output,  
confusion  
• High CO (rare) → fever, sepsis, hyperthyroidism  
❤ STROKE VOLUME (SV)  
 DEFINITION:  
• Amount of blood ejected from the left ventricle per beat.  
• Normal: 60–100 mL/beat.  
 DETERMINED BY:  
• Preload → volume returning to heart  
• Afterload → resistance against which heart pumps  
• Contractility → myocardial strength  
易 NCLEX LINK:  
• Hypovolemia → ↓ preload → ↓ SV → ↓ CO → ↓ BP (hypotension)  
• Fluid overload → ↑ preload → ↑ SV (to a limit; excess leads to HF)  
• Vasoconstriction → ↑ afterload → ↓ SV

易 NCLEX MNEMONICS  
Hypertension: “SILENT” → Stroke risk, Increased afterload, Left Ventricular  
hypertrophy, Eye damage, Nephropathy, TIA (Transient Ischemic Attack)  
Hypotension: “LOW” → Low fluids, Organ under-perfusion, Weak pulses  
Cardiac Output: “HR × SV = CO” — Think: “Heart Rate times Stroke Volume  
equals Cardiac Output”  
離 SAMPLE NCLEX QUESTIONS  
❓ A patient’s BP is 88/56 mmHg, HR 118 bpm, and urine output is  
15 mL/hr. What does this indicate?  
✅ Low cardiac output from hypotension (tachycardia is  
compensatory)  
❓ Which factor directly determines stroke volume?  
✅ Preload, afterload, and contractility  
❓ The nurse is teaching about hypertension prevention. Which  
instruction is correct?  
✅ Reduce sodium intake to ≤ 2,300 mg/day (DASH diet)
