變 CPR (CARDIOPULMONARY RESUSCITATION)  
 WHAT IS CPR?  
CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation) is an emergency life-saving procedure  
done when the heart stops beating (cardiac arrest). It helps maintain blood  
circulation and oxygenation to vital organs until advanced care is available.  
 KEY STEPS OF ADULT CPR (BLS GUIDELINES)  
• 留 Bystander CPR (Basic Life Support)  
• Check responsiveness: Tap and shout.  
• Call for help: Activate emergency response system (911/Code Blue).  
• Check breathing and pulse (no more than 10 seconds).  
◦ No pulse/breathing? Begin CPR  
 COMPRESSION GUIDELINES (CAB SEQUENCE)  
C – Circulation (Compressions)  
• Compression rate: 100–120/min  
• Compression depth: At least 2 inches (5 cm)  
• Allow full chest recoil  
• Minimize interruptions to <10 sec  
A – Airway  
• Head tilt-chin lift (unless spinal injury suspected)  
B – Breathing  
• Give 2 rescue breaths every 30 compressions  
• Each breath over 1 second with visible chest rise  
✅ NCLEX Tip: The ideal compression rhythm for CPR should match the beat  
of the song “Stayin’ Alive” by the Bee Gees —  100–120 beats per minute —  
which perfectly aligns with the recommended compression rate.  
 This fun and memorable association helps healthcare providers maintain  
effective chest compressions during CPR, a key factor in improving survival  
outcomes.

⚡ DEFIBRILLATION (AED USE (AUTOMATED  
EXTERNAL DEFIBRILLATOR))  
• Use as soon as available  
• Turn it on, apply pads, follow prompts  
• Shockable rhythms: Ventricular fibrillation (V-fib), pulseless V-tach  
• Non-shockable rhythms: Asystole, PEA (Pulseless Electrical Activity)  
 CPR NURSING PRIORITIES (NCLEX-FOCUSED)  
• Call for help FIRST (get defibrillator)  
• Start compressions immediately  
• High-quality compressions = best outcome  
• Rotate compressors every 2 minutes  
• Do not interrupt CPR for >10 seconds

Signs of ROSC:  
• Pulse returns

• BP improves

識 POST-RESUSCITATION CARE  
• Maintain oxygenation  
• Stabilize hemodynamics  
• Monitor neurologic function  
• Prepare for ICU transfer  
OLD - ❗You should NOT perform CPR if a fracture is suspected.  
NEW -❗You should NOT avoid CPR even if a fracture is suspected.  
Fractures (like rib or sternum fractures) are a known and acceptable risk  
during CPR and should not delay or prevent chest compressions in a cardiac  
arrest situation.  
領 What if the rib is already broken?  
• Yes, CPR can worsen rib fractures or even cause new ones.  
• A fractured rib may puncture a lung (leading to pneumothorax), but not  
doing CPR guarantees death from lack of oxygen.  
• The risk of injury is less important than restoring circulation and  
oxygen to the brain and heart.  
❗ NCLEX TIPS  
• No pulse + unresponsive → start compressions first  
• If patient has pulse but no breathing → rescue breaths only (1 every 5–6  
sec)  
• Check pulse every 2 minutes  
• CPR is priority over IV access or intubation  
• Always use firm, flat surface for compressions
