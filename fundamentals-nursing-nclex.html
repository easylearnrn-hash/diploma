🧠 FUNDAMENTALS OF NURSING

Safety is a top NCLEX priority because it protects both the patient and the nurse.

This comprehensive review covers the essential fundamentals every nursing student must master for NCLEX success.

🔒1. SAFETY & INFECTION CONTROL

🚧 FALL PRECAUTIONS

  • Use fall-risk bands (yellow wristband)
  • Keep bed in lowest position
  • Ensure call light is within reach
  • Use non-slip socks
  • Keep 2 side rails up (not 4 = restraint)
  • Assess orthostatic hypotension (drop in BP when standing)

🦠 STANDARD VS. TRANSMISSION-BASED PRECAUTIONS

🧠 Standard Precautions (used for all patients):
  • Hand hygiene before/after contact
  • Use gloves for blood/body fluids
  • Clean equipment between patients
  • Safe needle disposal

Transmission-Based Precautions:

Type Diseases PPE
Contact MRSA, C. diff Gloves + gown
Droplet Flu, Mumps Mask within 3 feet
Airborne TB, Measles, Chickenpox N95 mask + negative pressure room

🔑 Memory Tip: Airborne = Air = N95 mask

🧼 HAND HYGIENE (MOST TESTED!)

⚠️ CRITICAL:
  • Use soap & water for C. diff or visible dirt
  • Use alcohol-based hand rub in all other situations
  • Wash for 15–30 seconds

🩺2. VITAL SIGNS

Vital signs help detect early signs of deterioration.

Sign Normal Range
Temperature 97.8–99.1°F (36.5–37.3°C)
Heart Rate (HR) 60–100 bpm
Respiratory Rate (RR) 12–20 breaths/min
Blood Pressure (BP) 90/60–120/80 mmHg
Oxygen Saturation (SpO₂) 95–100%
Pain Always assess using 0–10 scale or faces chart
🚨 Report Immediately:
  • Temp >100.4°F (possible infection)
  • O₂ sat < 90% (hypoxemia = low oxygen in blood)

🧍3. PHYSICAL ASSESSMENT

Order of physical assessment: I-P-P-A

(Inspect → Palpate → Percuss → Auscultate)

🔁 Exception: Abdomen = Inspect → Auscultate → Percuss → Palpate

Why? Touching the abdomen first can cause false bowel sounds.

KEY ASSESSMENT TERMS:

  • Inspect = Look
  • Palpate = Feel with hands
  • Percuss = Tap to hear changes in sound (e.g., fluid vs air)
  • Auscultate = Use a stethoscope to listen (e.g., lungs, heart, bowel)

📋4. DOCUMENTATION

Nurses must document accurately and legally.

✅ DO:
  • Use objective facts: "Wound 2 cm with red edges"
  • Quote patients directly: "Patient states, 'I feel dizzy.'"
  • Document interventions and outcomes
  • Use black ink or EMR
❌ DON'T:
  • Write opinions: ("Patient seems lazy" ❌)
  • Leave blank spaces
  • Erase or use white-out
  • Document care you didn't do

Late entry? Label with date, time, and reason

🧠5. PATIENT RIGHTS & ETHICS

Principle Definition
Autonomy Right to make decisions
Beneficence Do good
Nonmaleficence Do no harm
Justice Fair treatment
Fidelity Keep promises
Veracity Tell the truth

🔒 INFORMED CONSENT (NCLEX MUST-KNOW!)

⚠️ CRITICAL RULES:
  • Only the provider explains procedure
  • RN can witness the signature
  • Patient must be competent & voluntary
  • ❌ No consent if patient is sedated, confused, or a minor (without guardian)

🗣6. COMMUNICATION & PATIENT TEACHING

THERAPEUTIC COMMUNICATION:

  • Use open-ended questions
  • Show empathy, active listening
  • Reflect or restate what the patient says
  • Use interpreter, not family, for language barriers
  • Avoid "Why" questions – may feel judgmental
💬 Examples:
  • "Tell me more about how you're feeling." ✅
  • "I understand this is hard for you." ✅

TEACHING TIPS:

  • Teach at 5th-grade level
  • Ask for teach-back to confirm understanding
  • Include family if needed

🧎7. POSITIONING – WHEN & WHY

Position Use For
Fowler's (45–60°) Promotes lung expansion
High Fowler's (90°) Dyspnea, eating, NG tube
Low Fowler's (15–30°) Post-op recovery
Supine Lying flat, post-op
Prone ARDS, COVID patients
Sims' Enemas, rectal meds
Trendelenburg Hypotension, shock
Lithotomy Pelvic exams

🎯 HIGH-YIELD NCLEX TIPS:
  • Safety first – Always prioritize airway, breathing, circulation (ABCs)
  • Hand hygiene is the #1 infection control measure
  • Assessment before action – Always assess before implementing
  • Never leave side rails all up – That's a restraint
  • Use teach-back method to verify patient understanding
  • Document objectively – Facts only, no opinions
