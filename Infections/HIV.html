🔴 HIV / AIDS

Human Immunodeficiency Virus — destroys CD4 T-cells, leading to AIDS (CD4 <200 or opportunistic infections)

🔬 Pathophysiology

  • HIV attacks CD4+ T-helper cells → immune system progressively destroyed
  • AIDS defined by: CD4 count <200 cells/µL OR presence of AIDS-defining opportunistic infection
  • HIV-1 = most common worldwide; HIV-2 = less virulent, mostly West Africa

🦠 Transmission

  • Blood-to-blood: IV drug use, needle sticks, blood transfusions
  • Sexual contact: vaginal, anal, oral
  • Vertical: mother-to-child (pregnancy, labor, breastfeeding)
  • NOT transmitted by: casual contact, saliva, tears, insect bites, toilet seats

📈 Stages of HIV Infection

StageDescriptionCD4 CountViral Load
Acute (Primary) HIV Flu-like illness 2–4 weeks after exposure ("mono-like"), most infectious Drops sharply Very high
Chronic Latency Asymptomatic; virus replicates slowly; can last 10+ years Slowly declining Low/undetectable
AIDS CD4 <200 OR opportunistic infections <200 Very high

🧪 Diagnosis

  • ELISA → screening test (sensitive)
  • Western Blot → confirmatory test (specific)
  • HIV RNA (viral load) → earliest detection (~10 days after exposure); monitors treatment response
  • CD4 count → disease staging (normal >500; AIDS <200)
  • OraQuick — rapid oral swab test (for community screening)
🧠 NCLEX KEY: First test = ELISA (screen); positive ELISA confirmed with Western Blot. HIV RNA detects virus earliest. CD4 tells you where the patient is in disease progression.

🧠 NCLEX KEY: ART is lifelong — patient must take EVERY dose. Missing doses = drug resistance. Zidovudine (AZT) → monitor bone marrow (CBC). ART also given to prevent vertical transmission (during pregnancy + labor).

💉 PEP and PrEP

PEP (Post-Exposure Prophylaxis)

  • Start within 72 hours of exposure
  • Take for 28 days
  • Needle sticks, unprotected sex, sexual assault

PrEP (Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis)

  • Truvada (tenofovir/emtricitabine) daily
  • For high-risk individuals
  • Must be HIV-negative before starting

🛡️ Isolation

  • Standard precautions only — HIV is NOT transmitted by casual contact
  • No special isolation room required
  • Protect HIV+ patients from infections (reverse precautions if severely immunocompromised)

⚡ NCLEX Must-Know

  • AIDS = CD4 <200 OR AIDS-defining OI
  • ELISA screens → Western Blot confirms
  • ART is lifelong — never skip doses → drug resistance
  • PCP prophylaxis: Bactrim (TMP-SMX) when CD4 <200
  • PEP within 72 hours of exposure (28 days)
  • Kaposi Sarcoma = purple lesions = HIV/AIDS + HHV-8
  • Zidovudine (AZT) → monitor CBC (bone marrow suppression)
  • Standard precautions only — no isolation needed
