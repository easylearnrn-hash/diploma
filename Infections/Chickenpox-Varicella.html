🐔 Chickenpox (Varicella)

Highly contagious viral infection caused by varicella-zoster virus (VZV)

🌬️ Transmission & Incubation

  • Airborne (respiratory droplets — tiny, long-range)
  • Direct contact with vesicle fluid
  • Contagious 1–2 days before rash and until all lesions crust over
  • Incubation: 10–21 days (typically 14–16 days)
🧠 NCLEX: Airborne precautions (NOT just droplet) + isolation until all lesions are crusted.

😷 Signs & Symptoms

Prodromal (before rash)

  • Fever, fatigue, headache, loss of appetite

Rash — 3 Classic Stages (Hallmark!)

  • Macules → Papules → Vesicles → Pustules → Crusting
  • Lesions in all stages simultaneously (pathognomonic)
  • Spreads from trunk → face → extremities
  • Intensely itchy
🧠 NCLEX: "Lesions in all stages of healing simultaneously" = chickenpox.

💊 Treatment

Routine / Healthy Children
  • Antihistamines for itching
  • Antipyretics — avoid aspirin (Reye's syndrome risk)
  • Oatmeal baths, calamine lotion
High-Risk → Antivirals
  • Acyclovir or valacyclovir
  • For: immunocompromised, adolescents/adults, pregnant women, neonates
⚠️ REYE'S SYNDROME: Never give aspirin to children with viral illness. Use acetaminophen/ibuprofen instead.

🛏 Infection Control

  • Airborne + Contact precautions
  • Negative-pressure private room
  • N95 mask for healthcare workers
  • Isolate until all lesions are crusted

💉 Varicella Vaccine

  • Live attenuated vaccine (2 doses)
  • 1st dose: 12–15 months | 2nd dose: 4–6 years
  • Contraindicated: pregnancy, severe immunosuppression

⚠️ Complications

ComplicationNCLEX Focus
Secondary bacterial infectionKeep skin clean; monitor for cellulitis
PneumoniaMonitor respiratory status (esp. adults)
EncephalitisAltered mental status, seizures
Reye's syndromeAvoid aspirin in children with viral illness
Herpes zoster (Shingles)Reactivation of dormant VZV later in life

⚡ NCLEX Must-Know Summary

  • Airborne + contact precautions → negative-pressure room
  • Isolate until ALL lesions are crusted (not just dried)
  • No aspirin → Reye's syndrome risk
  • Antivirals only for high-risk patients
  • Vaccine is live — don't give to pregnant/immunosuppressed
