🦟 Lyme Disease

Bacterial tick-borne infection caused by Borrelia burgdorferi

🦟 Transmission

  • Bite of infected black-legged (deer) tick
  • Tick must be attached ≥ 36–48 hours to transmit disease
  • Not spread person-to-person
🧠 NCLEX: Early tick removal (within 36 hr) prevents infection.

📊 Stages

StageTimingKey Signs
Stage 1 — Early LocalizedDays 3–30Erythema migrans (bull's-eye rash), flu-like symptoms
Stage 2 — Early DisseminatedWeeks–monthsFacial palsy (Bell's palsy), heart block (Lyme carditis), neurological symptoms
Stage 3 — LateMonths–yearsChronic arthritis (especially knees), memory loss, neuropathy
🧠 NCLEX: Bull's-eye rash = classic early Lyme. Not always present — can still have Lyme.

🧪 Diagnosis

  • Clinical — Erythema migrans + tick exposure history
  • ELISA → if positive → confirm with Western Blot
  • Note: early antibody tests can be false negative (takes weeks to develop antibodies)

💊 Treatment

StageAntibioticDuration
Early (Stage 1)Doxycycline (first-line); Amoxicillin or Cefuroxime10–21 days
Late/Neurological/Cardiac (Stage 2–3)IV Ceftriaxone2–4 weeks
🧠 NCLEX: Doxycycline NOT for pregnant women or children <8 → use Amoxicillin.

⚠️ Complications

  • Lyme arthritis — chronic joint swelling, especially knees
  • Bell's palsy — facial nerve drooping
  • Lyme carditis — heart block; may need temporary pacemaker
  • PTLDS (Post-Treatment Lyme Disease Syndrome) — fatigue + joint pain even after treatment

🛡️ Prevention

  • Long sleeves and pants in tick-infested areas
  • DEET or permethrin repellent
  • Full-body tick check after outdoors
  • Remove tick with tweezers — grasp close to skin, pull straight out
  • Shower within 2 hours of outdoor activity

⚡ NCLEX Rapid Fire

  • Tick attached <36 hr → likely no disease
  • Bull's-eye rash = erythema migrans = Lyme
  • First test: ELISA → confirm with Western Blot
  • First-line drug: Doxycycline (not for pregnant/kids <8)
  • Cardiac involvement → may need temporary pacemaker
