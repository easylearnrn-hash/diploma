🦠 Clostridium difficile (C. diff)

Healthcare-associated infectious colitis — gram-positive, spore-forming, anaerobic bacterium

💥 Cause & Risk Factors

C. diff overgrows when normal gut flora is disrupted — most commonly by antibiotic use.

  • Antibiotics that disrupt flora: clindamycin, fluoroquinolones, cephalosporins
  • Hospitalization or long-term care
  • Age >65
  • Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs)
  • Immunocompromised patients

🚨 Signs & Symptoms

  • Profuse watery diarrhea (≥3 loose stools/24 hr)
  • Fever, abdominal cramping
  • Foul-smelling stools
  • Leukocytosis (↑ WBC), dehydration

Severe Complications:

  • Pseudomembranous colitis
  • Toxic megacolon (dilated colon → can rupture)
  • Sepsis

🧪 Diagnosis

  • Stool sample → test for C. diff toxins A and B
  • PCR — detects C. diff DNA
  • Enzyme immunoassay (EIA)

💊 Treatment

First-line antibiotics:

  • Oral Vancomycin (preferred)
  • Fidaxomicin — narrow spectrum, preserves flora
  • Metronidazole — only if others unavailable

AVOID:

  • Antidiarrheals (loperamide/Imodium) → risk of toxic megacolon
  • Unnecessary antibiotics

🧼 Infection Control (NCLEX PRIORITY)

  • Contact precautions — gown + gloves
  • Private room
  • Handwashing with soap and water — alcohol-based sanitizers do NOT kill spores
  • Clean environment with bleach (sporicidal disinfectant)
  • Continue precautions until diarrhea resolves and stools are formed
🧠 NCLEX KEY: C. diff = soap and water, NOT alcohol gel. Alcohol does not kill spores.

⚡ NCLEX Must-Know

  • Profuse watery, foul-smelling diarrhea after antibiotic use → think C. diff
  • Isolate immediately — contact precautions
  • Wash hands with soap + water (not hand sanitizer)
  • Oral Vancomycin preferred treatment
  • No antidiarrheals — can cause toxic megacolon
  • Monitor hydration and electrolytes
