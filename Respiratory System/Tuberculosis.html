Respiratory System

🦠 Tuberculosis (TB)

Airborne precautions, latent vs active, TB RISK + TB SEEDS mnemonics, RIPE/PERI treatment

Overview
TB = Mycobacterium tuberculosis — acid-fast, aerobic bacterium. Primary site: lung apex (high O₂ environment). Can spread to brain, spine, kidneys, joints, lymph nodes. Spread by airborne transmission — NOT droplet!

Transmission & Infection Control

🚨 Airborne Precautions (NOT Droplet!)

  • N95 respirator — for all healthcare workers entering the room
  • Negative pressure isolation room — door must remain closed
  • Surgical mask on patient when leaving the room
  • Teach cough etiquette; avoid crowded places
NCLEX Critical: TB = AIRBORNE precautions → N95 mask + negative-pressure room. This is frequently tested and easily confused with droplet precautions (used for flu, COVID, meningitis).

PPD Positive Criteria

Induration Size Thresholds

  • ≥5 mm — HIV-positive, recent contact with TB patient, chest X-ray suggestive of old TB, organ transplant or immunosuppressed (on steroids)
  • ≥10 mm — Recent immigrants (past 5 years), IV drug users, high-risk settings (prisons, healthcare), children <4 or exposed children
  • ≥15 mm — Healthy individuals with no known risk factors

False Positive

  • Previous BCG vaccine (common in foreign-born patients)

False Negative

  • Immunosuppression
  • Recent TB infection (<8 weeks)
  • Very young or elderly
  • Viral illness
TB Treatment — Mnemonic: RIPE / PERI
DrugSide EffectsMonitor
R — RifampinOrange/red body fluids (urine, tears, sweat), hepatotoxicity, ↓ effectiveness of birth controlLiver function tests; advise condom use
I — Isoniazid (INH)Peripheral neuropathy, hepatotoxicityGive Vitamin B6 (pyridoxine) to prevent neuropathy; LFTs
P — Pyrazinamide↑ Uric acid → gout, hepatotoxicityUric acid levels, LFTs

Duration: 6–12 months total. Biggest risk = hepatotoxicity (all first-line drugs). Biggest patient education point = medication adherence to prevent drug resistance.
NCLEX Tip: Patient on Ethambutol reports blurred vision or trouble distinguishing colors → notify provider immediately — this is optic neuritis!
Priority Nursing Actions

Active TB — Priority Order

  1. Initiate airborne precautions — N95 + negative pressure room (FIRST action)
  2. Collect sputum BEFORE starting antibiotics
  3. Start RIPE regimen as ordered
  4. Monitor respiratory status and O₂ saturation
  5. Monitor medication side effects (vision, liver function, neuropathy)
  6. Educate patient on isolation criteria

Latent TB

  • Screen high-risk patients with PPD or IGRA
  • Treat with INH × 6–9 months to prevent activation
  • Give Vitamin B6 with INH to prevent peripheral neuropathy
  • Monitor liver function tests regularly
When to Discontinue Isolation?

ALL 3 Criteria Must Be Met:

  • ✅ ≥3 weeks of effective TB medications
  • ✅ 3 consecutive negative sputum cultures
  • ✅ Clinical improvement of symptoms
Patient Education

Key Teaching Points

  • Adhere strictly to medications — missed doses → drug-resistant TB (MDR-TB)
  • Avoid alcohol and hepatotoxic drugs
  • High-protein, high-calorie diet (combat weight loss)
  • Screen exposed family members
  • Report worsening cough, hemoptysis, fever, weight loss
  • Rifampin turns body fluids orange — this is expected, not harmful (warn patient)
NCLEX Must-Know

Top NCLEX Points

  • First nursing action = airborne precautions + isolation room
  • Most definitive diagnostic test = sputum culture
  • PPD measures induration (hardness), NOT redness
  • Read PPD at 48–72 hours
  • Biggest medication risk = hepatotoxicity
  • Ethambutol → optic neuritis → report visual changes immediately
  • INH → give Vitamin B6 to prevent peripheral neuropathy
  • Rifampin = orange fluids + decreases birth control effectiveness
  • Collect sputum BEFORE antibiotics
  • BCG vaccine causes false-positive PPD → use IGRA instead
