🩺 VITAL SIGNS INTERPRETATION

Vital signs are the most fundamental assessment data nurses collect. They provide critical information about a patient's physiological status and can indicate early signs of deterioration.

Remember: Always compare to the patient's baseline and assess trends over time, not just single readings.

📊NORMAL RANGES

Vital Sign Normal Range
Temperature 97.7–99.5°F (36.5–37.5°C)
Heart Rate (HR) 60–100 bpm (beats per minute)
Respiratory Rate (RR) 12–20 breaths per minute
Blood Pressure (BP) 90/60 to 120/80 mmHg
Oxygen Saturation (SpO₂) 95–100%

AGE-BASED DIFFERENCES

👶 INFANTS:
  • ↑ HR & RR (higher heart and respiratory rates)
  • ↓ BP (lower blood pressure)
👴 OLDER ADULTS:
  • ↓ temperature baseline (may not show fever even with infection)
  • May have lower HR at rest
  • More prone to orthostatic hypotension

🚨PRIORITY ACTIONS

⚠️ BP <90/60 = RISK FOR HYPOPERFUSION

(Inadequate blood flow to organs)

  • Assess: Mental status, urine output, skin color/temperature
  • Intervene: Initiate fluids if ordered, notify provider
  • Monitor: Continuous assessment for shock symptoms
🌡️ TEMP >100.4°F = POSSIBLE INFECTION
  • Assess: WBC count, source of infection
  • Cultures: Blood, urine, wound cultures as ordered
  • Start antibiotics as prescribed (after cultures collected!)
💓 HR <60 OR >100 = CHECK PERFUSION
  • Bradycardia (<60): Hold medications like beta blockers, assess for symptoms
  • Tachycardia (>100): Assess for pain, anxiety, fever, dehydration, bleeding
  • Always: Check capillary refill, level of consciousness, blood pressure

😖PAIN ASSESSMENT

Pain is considered the "5th vital sign" and must be assessed regularly.

PAIN SCALES

📊 NUMERIC SCALE (0–10)

Use for: Adults and older children

How it works:

  • 0 = No pain
  • 1-3 = Mild pain
  • 4-6 = Moderate pain
  • 7-10 = Severe pain

😊😢 WONG-BAKER FACES SCALE

Use for: Children ages 3+ and patients with language barriers

How it works: Patient points to the face that matches their pain level (happy face to crying face)

👶 FLACC SCALE

Use for: Infants, non-verbal patients, cognitively impaired

Assesses 5 behaviors:

  • Face
  • Legs
  • Activity
  • Cry
  • Consolability

Each scored 0-2, total score 0-10

TYPES OF PAIN

⚡ ACUTE PAIN

Characteristics: Sudden onset, short-term duration

Examples:

  • Post-operative pain
  • Injury or trauma
  • Medical procedures

Nursing Goals:

  • Pain relief: Administer medications as ordered
  • Monitor side effects: Watch for respiratory depression with opioids
  • Assess effectiveness: Reassess pain 30-60 minutes after intervention

🔄 CHRONIC PAIN

Characteristics: Ongoing, lasts >3-6 months

Examples:

  • Arthritis
  • Fibromyalgia
  • Neuropathy
  • Chronic back pain

Nursing Goals:

  • Improve function: Focus on ADLs and quality of life
  • Coping strategies: Teach relaxation, distraction, heat/cold therapy
  • Long-term management: Non-pharmacologic approaches + medications

🛠️TYPES OF NURSING INTERVENTIONS

Understanding which interventions require orders versus independent nursing judgment is crucial for NCLEX and practice.

✅ INDEPENDENT INTERVENTIONS

RN can do alone (no provider order needed)

Examples: Turning/repositioning patients, patient education, fall risk assessment, basic comfort measures, hand hygiene

📋 DEPENDENT INTERVENTIONS

Require provider order

Examples: Administering medications, starting oxygen therapy, ordering labs/diagnostics, inserting urinary catheters

🤝 COLLABORATIVE INTERVENTIONS

Work with other disciplines

Examples: Physical therapy for mobility, dietitian for nutrition plan, social worker for discharge planning, respiratory therapy for breathing treatments

🕒TIME MANAGEMENT & SHIFT PRIORITIZATION

COMMON NCLEX QUESTION STYLES

❓ WHO TO SEE FIRST?

Use these frameworks:

  • ABCs: Airway, Breathing, Circulation
  • Maslow's Hierarchy: Physiological needs first
  • Unstable vs Stable: See unstable patients first
  • Acute vs Chronic: Acute/sudden changes take priority
🔄 WHAT TO DELEGATE?

LPNs/LVNs can:

  • Care for stable patients
  • Routine care and monitoring
  • Administer medications (no IV push in most states)
  • Wound care and dressing changes

UAPs (Unlicensed Assistive Personnel) can:

  • ADLs (bathing, feeding, toileting)
  • Vital signs (for stable patients)
  • Ambulation and transfers
  • Basic hygiene care
📝 WHAT TO DOCUMENT NOW VS LATER?

Document immediately:

  • Critical changes in patient status
  • Abnormal assessments
  • Medication administrations
  • Provider notifications
  • Patient/family teaching

🧠NURSING PROCESS (ADPIE)

The nursing process is a systematic approach to patient care that guides all nursing actions.

Step What It Means
Assessment Collect data (subjective & objective)
Diagnosis Identify nursing diagnosis (NANDA)
Planning Set goals and expected outcomes
Implementation Take action, carry out interventions
Evaluation Was the goal met? Reassess as needed
🧠 NCLEX TIP:

Never jump to implementation before assessing! Always assess first unless the patient is in immediate danger (e.g., choking, cardiac arrest).

🚧FALL RISK & SAFETY MEASURES

HIGH-RISK FACTORS

  • History of falls
  • Age >65
  • Medications: Sedatives, antihypertensives, opioids
  • Cognitive impairment: Dementia, confusion
  • Gait or balance issues
  • Sensory deficits: Vision/hearing problems

FALL PREVENTION INTERVENTIONS

✅ SAFETY MEASURES:
  • Use bed alarms for high-risk patients
  • Place call light within reach
  • Keep frequently used items at bedside
  • Provide non-slip socks
  • Keep bed in lowest position
  • Use 2–3 side rails (not all 4 → considered a restraint!)
  • Reassess fall risk regularly (every shift)

🎯 NCLEX HIGH-YIELD TIPS:
  • Vital signs: Always compare to baseline, look for trends
  • Pain assessment: Believe the patient — pain is subjective
  • Delegation: RNs assess, LPNs implement stable care, UAPs do ADLs
  • Prioritization: Unstable > stable, ABCs always first
  • Documentation: If it's not documented, it didn't happen
  • Safety: 2-3 side rails = safe; 4 side rails = restraint
  • Nursing process: Never skip assessment unless life-threatening emergency
