Why Choose ACNHS

System-Based Curriculum

Our curriculum is structured by body systems and clinical conditions, not memorization. This approach mirrors real clinical practice and aligns directly with NCLEX-RN clinical judgment and decision-making frameworks, preparing students to think like nurses from day one.

Clinical-First Training

Students train in high-fidelity simulation labs, OSCE-style assessments, and supervised hospital placements. Clinical competence is developed early and reinforced continuously, ensuring graduates are practice-ready, not just exam-ready.

Global Mobility

Programs are designed to meet international nursing education standards, with transcripts structured for global evaluation and mobility. Graduates are prepared for opportunities both locally and internationally, subject to regulatory approval.

Strict Academic Standards

ACNHS enforces clear attendance policies, grading standards, and professional conduct requirements. This discipline reflects real healthcare expectations and ensures graduates meet the ethical, academic, and professional standards required of registered nurses.

Frequently Asked Questions

U.S. Nursing Licensure for International Students

Is ACNHS approved by the U.S. State Board of Nursing?

No. ACNHS is an Armenian institution accredited in the Republic of Armenia by the Ministry of Education, Science, Culture & Sport (MESCS). It is not approved by any U.S. State Board of Nursing, nor is it a U.S.-based program.

To become a Registered Nurse (RN) in the United States, you must obtain licensure from a specific U.S. State Board of Nursing (e.g., California Board of Registered Nursing, New York State Board of Nursing). Each state board independently reviews and approves nursing education programs within its jurisdiction.

Can I take the NCLEX with an ACNHS transcript?

It depends. The NCLEX (National Council Licensure Examination) is the standardized exam required for U.S. RN licensure. Eligibility to sit for the NCLEX is determined by the individual State Board of Nursing to which you apply.

Most state boards require a credentials evaluation from an approved agency (e.g., CGFNS International, IES, IERF) to verify that your foreign nursing education is equivalent to U.S. standards. The state board then decides whether your credentials meet their specific requirements.

Important: NCLEX eligibility is not automatic for international graduates. Requirements vary significantly by state.

What is a foreign credential evaluation?

A foreign credential evaluation is an independent assessment of your nursing education by a U.S. credentials evaluation agency. Commonly used agencies include:

  • CGFNS International (Commission on Graduates of Foreign Nursing Schools)
  • IES (International Education Services)
  • IERF (International Education Research Foundation)

These agencies review your transcripts, course content, and clinical training to determine if your education is comparable to a U.S. nursing program. Most state boards require this evaluation as part of the licensure application process.

Is ACNHS accredited by ACEN or CCNE?

No. ACEN (Accreditation Commission for Education in Nursing) and CCNE (Commission on Collegiate Nursing Education) are U.S.-based nursing program accreditors that only evaluate nursing programs located in the United States.

ACNHS is an Armenian institution and therefore not eligible for ACEN or CCNE accreditation. Instead, ACNHS is accredited by the Armenian Ministry of Education, Science, Culture & Sport (MESCS) and operates under Armenian higher education standards.

For U.S. licensure purposes, state boards evaluate foreign programs through credentials evaluation agencies, not through ACEN/CCNE.

Which U.S. states accept foreign nursing graduates?

All 50 U.S. states and territories allow internationally educated nurses (IENs) to apply for licensure, but each state has unique requirements. Some states have more streamlined processes for IENs, while others have additional requirements (e.g., specific clinical hour minimums, coursework in U.S. healthcare law).

State boards with historically more IEN-friendly processes include:

  • New York
  • California
  • Florida
  • Texas
  • Illinois

You must research the specific requirements of the state where you intend to practice. Visit the state board's website or consult a credentials evaluation agency.

What is the process to become a U.S. RN as an ACNHS graduate?

  1. Complete your nursing education at ACNHS (diploma or degree program)
  2. Choose a U.S. state where you want to become licensed
  3. Submit your transcripts to a credentials evaluation agency (e.g., CGFNS, IES, IERF)
  4. Obtain credentials evaluation report confirming equivalency to U.S. education
  5. Apply to the State Board of Nursing in your chosen state for NCLEX eligibility
  6. Meet additional state requirements (English proficiency tests, background checks, state-specific coursework)
  7. Receive Authorization to Test (ATT) from the state board
  8. Schedule and pass the NCLEX-RN exam
  9. Receive your RN license from the state board (if you pass NCLEX and meet all other requirements)

Timeframe: This process typically takes 6-18 months after graduation, depending on the state and how quickly you complete each step.

Does ACNHS guarantee U.S. licensure?

No. ACNHS cannot guarantee that graduates will be eligible for U.S. RN licensure. Licensure eligibility is determined solely by individual State Boards of Nursing, which independently evaluate each applicant's credentials.

State board decisions are based on:

  • The credentials evaluation report
  • State-specific education and clinical hour requirements
  • English language proficiency
  • Background checks and other state policies

ACNHS provides: A comprehensive nursing education aligned with international standards, official transcripts documenting your coursework and clinical experience, and support in preparing you for the NCLEX exam content areas.

Your responsibility: Research state board requirements, complete the credentials evaluation process, and meet all state-specific eligibility criteria.

Does ACNHS provide NCLEX preparation?

Yes. ACNHS curriculum is designed with NCLEX content categories in mind:

  • Safe & Effective Care Environment
  • Health Promotion & Maintenance
  • Psychosocial Integrity
  • Physiological Integrity

Our courses cover the knowledge domains tested on the NCLEX-RN, including medical-surgical nursing, pharmacology, maternal-child health, mental health, and critical care.

However: ACNHS is not an NCLEX test prep course. You should plan to use additional NCLEX review resources (e.g., UWorld, Kaplan, Saunders NCLEX-RN books) to specifically prepare for the exam format and question styles.

Who can I contact with more questions?

For questions about U.S. licensure eligibility and the credentials evaluation process, we recommend contacting:

  • CGFNS International: www.cgfns.org
  • Specific U.S. State Board of Nursing: Visit NCSBN.org to find your state board contact info
